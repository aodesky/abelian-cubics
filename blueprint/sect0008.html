9 Comparison with Santens

We use the minimal resolution \(\pi :X\to S\) of the preceding chapter. The proposition first specifies the integral points and height. Its proof verifies the hypotheses of Santens’s theorem and then computes the leading constant.

For \(s\in \mathbb {C}\), write \(H(P,s)\) for the toric height of \(P\) with respect to \(sD_0\) and \(H_v(P,s)\) for its local factor at a place \(v\). Thus \(H(P,1)=H(P)\) is the height of Corollary 1, which equals \(\sqrt{1-3a}\) at an integral point with characteristic polynomial \(t^3-t^2+at+b\) (Proposition 2). The constant \(\alpha \) is defined in Equation 1.

Let \(\pi \colon X\to S\) be the minimal resolution, let \(D\subset X\) be the reduced preimage of \(D_0\), and put \(U=X\setminus D\). The coordinate ring of the affine chart \(\mathscr {S}\setminus \mathscr {D}_0\) pulls back to a finitely generated \(\mathbb {Z}\)-subalgebra

\[ A:=\pi ^*\Gamma (\mathscr {S}\setminus \mathscr {D}_0,\mathcal O) \subset \Gamma (U,\mathcal O). \]

A point \(P\in U(\mathbb {Q})\) is integral if \(g(P)\in \mathbb {Z}\) for every \(g\in A\). With this integral condition and the height \(H(\pi (-),1)\), [ 28 , Theorem 8.7 ] gives

\[ \# \{ P\in (\mathscr {S}\setminus \mathscr {D}_0)(\mathbb {Z}):H(P,1)\leq B\} \sim cB^2\log B,\qquad c=\frac{2\alpha }{9}. \]
Proof in Lean ▼

Santens’s formula has the shape \(cB^a(\log B)^{b-1}\); we check his hypotheses, find \(a=b=2\), and compute \(c\). Every integral point of \(\mathscr {S}\setminus \mathscr {D}_0\) lies in the torus \(T\), over which \(\pi \) is an isomorphism, so the integral points of \(U\) correspond to \((\mathscr {S}\setminus \mathscr {D}_0)(\mathbb {Z})\). In the fan of Proposition 11, the toric divisors in \(U\) belong to \(v_1,v_2,(1,0),(0,1)\); a character with order \(0\) along all of them is trivial, so \(U\) has only constant units. The four exceptional components of \(D\) have no real points, so the only real boundary component is the strict transform \(D_0'\) of \(D_0\). Removing the four exceptional components from \(X\) leaves an open set whose real points form the compact set \(X(\mathbb {R})\) and whose regular functions are constant, because \(v_0+v_1+v_2=0\). The integral condition is encoded by local indicator functions \(f_p\) of \(\pi ^{-1}((\mathscr {S}\setminus \mathscr {D}_0)(\mathbb {Z}_p))\), which have compact open support by properness.

In Santens’s group \(\operatorname {Pic}(X;\{ D_0'\} )\), write \(R=D_{v_1}+D_{v_2}\) and \(H'=D_{(1,0)}+D_{(0,1)}\). The divisor of \(n=\nu \overline\nu \) (with \(\nu \) the image of \(P\) under the isomorphism \(\iota \) of Proposition 3) gives the single relation \(R+H'=2[D_0']\), and the effective cone is spanned by \(R\) and \(H'\). The height \(H(\cdot ,1)\) is a height for \(L=[D_0']\), and \(-K_{(X;\{ D_0'\} )}=R+H'=2L\) with rank \(2\), so \(a=b=2\).

Santens requires local norms with values in \(p^{\mathbb {Z}}\cup \{ 0\} \) at each prime [ 28 , Definition 4.1 ] , and \(H(\cdot ,1)\) does not satisfy this. We therefore apply his theorem to the height with the trivial metric on \(L\) at every prime (the canonical section of \(\mathcal{O}(D_0')\) has norm \(1\)) and the metric of \(H(\cdot ,1)\) at the real place. At every point where some \(f_p\) is nonzero, \(n\) and \(\nu ^2\overline\nu \) are integral, so \(H_p(P,1)=1\) by Equation 48; the two heights agree on the support of the weight and give the same count and constant.

The dual-cone factor is \(\theta =\int _0^\infty \int _0^{2y_1}e^{-y_1}\, dy_2\, dy_1=2\). Since \(\operatorname {Pic}U_{\overline{\mathbb {Q}}}\cong \mathbb {Z}[\operatorname {Gal}(E/\mathbb {Q})]\), its \(L\)-function is \(\xi \) and its first Galois cohomology vanishes by Shapiro’s lemma, so the Brauer factor is \(1\). Equation 47 then gives \(c=2\rho _E\prod _v\tau '_v\). The local factors are \(1-3p^{-2}+2p^{-3}\) for \(p\equiv 1\pmod3\) (there are \(3k+1\) valuation pairs with \(v_p(n)=k\)), \(1-p^{-2}\) for \(p\equiv 2\pmod3\), \(2/(27\sqrt3)\) at \(3\) (where integrality means \(\nu \in 1+3\mathcal{O}_{E,3}\) by Proposition 4), and \(2\pi \) at the real place (the residue measure on the circle \(D_0'(\mathbb {R})\)). Using \(\prod _{p\neq 3}\tau '_p=27\alpha /(4\pi ^2)\) and \(\rho _E=\pi /(3\sqrt3)\) gives \(c=2\alpha /9\). This is nonzero, which is the last hypothesis of Santens’s theorem.

The proof assumes Santens’s theorem (Cited theorem 1).

In the proof of Proposition 12, Santens’s exponents are \(a=b=2\) and the dual-cone factor is \(\theta =2\). Write \(\rho _E=\operatorname {Res}_{s=1}\xi (s)\). For a finite prime \(p\), \(\tau _p^\prime \) is the local volume of the integral points multiplied by \(L_p(1,\operatorname {Pic}U_{\overline{\mathbb {Q}}})^{-1}\); \(\tau _\infty ^\prime \) is the residue volume of the real boundary component. The Brauer factor is one.

\begin{equation*} c=\frac{\theta }{a(b-1)!}\, \tau =\tau ,\qquad \tau =2\rho _E\prod _v\tau ’_v, \end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

With Brauer factor \(1\), [ 28 , Theorem 6.9, Lemma 5.24 ] gives \(c=\theta \tau /(a(b-1)!)\), and \(\theta =2\), \(a=b=2\) make the prefactor \(1\). The Tamagawa number \(\tau \) has the factor \(c_{\mathbf A}=2\) from the single real boundary component [ 28 , Definition 4.21 ] , the residue \(\rho _E\) of \(L(s,\operatorname {Pic}U_{\overline{\mathbb {Q}}})=\xi (s)\), and the local factors \(\tau '_v\). We use Peyre’s convergence factors \(L_p(1,\operatorname {Pic}U_{\overline{\mathbb {Q}}})^{-1}\) [ 27 , § 2 ] ; with the reciprocal factors printed in version 2 of Santens’s Definition 4.21, the product over split primes would diverge.

For \(P\in T(\mathbb {Q}_v)\), put \(\nu =\iota (P)\in (E\otimes _\mathbb {Q}\mathbb {Q}_v)^\times \), with \(\iota \) the isomorphism of Proposition 3, and \(n=\nu \overline\nu \). Each \(|\cdot |_w\) is applied to the \(w\)-component, as in Equation 11. The local height of \(P\) for \(3D_0\) is given by the following formula.

\begin{equation*} H_v(P,3)=\prod _{w\mid v} \max \bigl(1,|n|_w,|\nu ^2\overline\nu |_w, |\nu \overline\nu ^2|_w\bigr)^{1/2}. \end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

Start from the local height of Equation 11 with \(e_2=a\) and \(e_3=-b\), where \(t^3-t^2+at+b\) is the characteristic polynomial of \(P\). By Proposition 3, \(n=1-3a\), which is the second entry. The last two entries have sum \(2-9a-27b=\operatorname {Tr}_{E/\mathbb {Q}}(\nu ^2\overline\nu )\) and product \(n^3=\operatorname {Nm}(\nu ^2\overline\nu )\), so they are \(\nu ^2\overline\nu \) and \(\nu \overline\nu ^2\). Taking the product over the places \(w\mid v\) gives the formula.
