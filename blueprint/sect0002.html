2 The orbit parametrization

A \(C_3\)-algebra is either a cyclic cubic field with a specified \(C_3\)-action, or \(\mathbb {Q}^3\) with cyclic permutation of its factors. An element is normal when its three conjugates form a \(\mathbb {Q}\)-basis. We identify pairs \((K,x)\) up to \(C_3\)-equivariant \(\mathbb {Q}\)-algebra isomorphism taking one distinguished element to the other.

Theorem 5 Rational points of the moduli space
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.OrbitParametrization.ModularInterpretation.Claim
  • Cubic.OrbitParametrization.C3Algebra.«thm:modularInterpretation»

There is a bijection between rational points of \(T\) and equivalence classes of \(C_3\)-algebras \(K/\mathbb {Q}\) equipped with a trace-one normal element \(x\in K\).

Proof in Lean ▼

Let \(\mathcal G_1\) be the torus of augmentation-one units of the group algebra of \(C_3\), so that \(T=\mathcal G_1/C_3\), and let \(A\) and \(A^{C_3}\) be the coordinate rings of \(\mathcal G_1\) and \(T\). We follow [ 26 , § 2 ] . A pair \((K,x)\) gives the element \(u=\sum _{g\in C_3}(g^{-1}x)[g]\) of the group algebra \(K[C_3]\). Its augmentation is \(\operatorname {Tr}(x)=1\), and \(u\) is a unit if and only if \(x\) is normal. Thus \(u\) is a \(K\)-point of \(\mathcal G_1\); its image in \(T\) is fixed by the action of \(C_3\) on \(K\), so it is a rational point, and its characteristic polynomial is that of \(x\). Conversely, a rational point \(P\) of \(T\) gives the fibre algebra \(A\otimes _{A^{C_3},P}\mathbb {Q}\) with its \(C_3\)-action. It is a \(C_3\)-algebra, and the image of the first coordinate is a trace-one normal element. If \((K,x)\) maps to \(P\), the equivariant map from the fibre algebra to \(K\) sending the first coordinate to \(x\) carries the coordinate basis to the basis of conjugates of \(x\). So it is an isomorphism of pairs, and pairs with the same point are equivalent. Equivalent pairs give the same point, and the fibre pair of \(P\) maps to \(P\), so the map is a bijection. Lean formalizes this rational-point correspondence, not the moduli statement over general bases.

Example 1 A cyclic cubic field and its twist
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.OrbitParametrization.TwistedCubicField.Claim
  • Cubic.OrbitParametrization.C3Algebra.«example:twoRationalPointsSamePoly»

Let \(K\) be a cubic abelian number field. Then \(K\), equipped with its canonical Galois action, is a \(C_3\)-algebra. The twist \(K'\) of the \(C_3\)-algebra \(K\) by the outer automorphism \(g \mapsto g^{-1}\) of \(C_3\) (with twisted action \(g \ast x = g^{-1}x\)) is not isomorphic to \(K\) as a \(C_3\)-algebra. 1

Proof in Lean ▼

Since \(K/\mathbb {Q}\) is Galois of degree \(3\), an identification of \(C_3\) with \(\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb {Q})\) makes \(K\) a \(C_3\)-algebra. Suppose \(\tau :K\to K'\) is an isomorphism of \(C_3\)-algebras. Then \(\tau \) is a field automorphism of \(K\) with \(\tau (gy)=g^{-1}\tau (y)\) for all \(g\in C_3\) and \(y\in K\). But \(\tau \) lies in the abelian group \(\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb {Q})\), so it commutes with \(g\), and hence \(g\) and \(g^{-1}\) act in the same way. This fails for a generator, since the action is faithful. For the footnote, the cocycle of \(K\) is the surjective homomorphism \(c:\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb {Q}}/\mathbb {Q})\to C_3\) given by restriction to \(K\), and the cocycle of \(K'\) is \(c^{-1}\). Since \(C_3\) is abelian, cohomologous cocycles are equal, and \(c\neq c^{-1}\) because \(c\) is surjective. Embedding \(C_3\) in \(S_3\) by its action on itself, inversion on \(C_3\) is conjugation by a transposition, so the images in \(H^1(\mathbb {Q},S_3)\) agree.

Example 2 The split algebra and its twist
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.OrbitParametrization.SplitAlgebraTwist.Claim
  • Cubic.OrbitParametrization.C3Algebra.«example:splitAlgebra»

Let \(K_{\text{spl}} = \mathbb {Q}^3\), the split cubic algebra. Then \(C_3\subset S_3 = \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb {Q}\text{-alg}}(K_{\text{spl}})\) and \(K_{\text{spl}}\), equipped with its canonical \(C_3\)-action, is a \(C_3\)-algebra. Any transposition gives an isomorphism of \(C_3\)-algebras from \(K_{\text{spl}}\) to its twist \(K_{\text{spl}}'\).

Proof in Lean ▼

A \(\mathbb {Q}\)-algebra automorphism of \(\mathbb {Q}^3\) permutes its three primitive idempotents, so \(\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb {Q}\text{-alg}}(\mathbb {Q}^3)=S_3\), acting by permuting coordinates. The cyclic permutations form \(C_3\), whose fixed algebra is \(\mathbb {Q}\), so \(K_{\text{spl}}\) is a \(C_3\)-algebra. Index the coordinates by \(\mathbb {Z}/3\mathbb {Z}\), with \(g\in C_3\) acting by translation. A transposition is a reflection \(h\mapsto c-h\), and it turns translation by \(g\) into translation by \(-g\). Hence permuting the coordinates by a transposition is an isomorphism \(K_{\text{spl}}\to K_{\text{spl}}'\) that intertwines \(g\) with \(g^{-1}\).

Example 3 Normal elements of the split algebra
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.OrbitParametrization.SplitNormalElements.Claim
  • Cubic.OrbitParametrization.C3Algebra.«example:splitAlgebraNormalElement»

An element \(x\) of trace one in the split \(C_3\)-algebra \(K_{\text{spl}}\) is normal if and only if \(x\) either has distinct coordinates or exactly two identical coordinates. For such a normal element \(x\), the pairs \((K_{\text{spl}},x)\) and \((K_{\text{spl}}',x)\) are equivalent if and only if \(x\) has exactly two identical coordinates (swapping the identical coordinates gives the required isomorphism); in particular, if \(x\) has distinct coordinates then \((K_{\text{spl}},x)\) and \((K_{\text{spl}}',x)\) determine different rational points of \(\mathcal G_1/C_3\), even though \(K_{\text{spl}}\) and \(K_{\text{spl}}'\) are isomorphic as \(C_3\)-algebras.

Proof in Lean ▼

The conjugates of \(x=(x_0,x_1,x_2)\) are its cyclic shifts. With \(e_1=x_0+x_1+x_2=1\) and \(e_2=x_0x_1+x_1x_2+x_2x_0\), we have \(e_1^2-3e_2=\frac12\sum _{i{\lt}j}(x_i-x_j)^2\). By Lemma 9, \(x\) is normal exactly when its coordinates are not all equal, that is, when they are distinct or exactly two agree. The twist has the same conjugates of \(x\), so \(x\) is also a trace-one normal element of \(K_{\text{spl}}'\). An equivalence \((K_{\text{spl}},x)\to (K_{\text{spl}}',x)\) is an automorphism \(e\) of \(\mathbb {Q}^3\) with \(e(gy)=g^{-1}e(y)\) and \(e(x)=x\). Composing \(e\) with the reflection \(h\mapsto -h\) of the coordinates gives a \(C_3\)-equivariant automorphism of \(K_{\text{spl}}\), which is a cyclic shift, since the centralizer of \(C_3\) in \(S_3\) is \(C_3\). So \(e\) is a reflection \(h\mapsto c-h\), which swaps two coordinates and fixes the third, and \(e(x)=x\) exactly when the two swapped coordinates of \(x\) agree. Conversely, if two coordinates agree, the transposition swapping them is an isomorphism to the twist (Example 2) and fixes \(x\). If \(x\) has distinct coordinates, the two pairs are inequivalent, so they give different rational points by Theorem 5, while \(K_{\text{spl}}\cong K_{\text{spl}}'\) by Example 2.

  1. In terms of Galois cohomology, the non-cohomologous \(1\)-cocycles in \(H^1(\mathbb {Q},C_3)\) corresponding to the \(C_3\)-algebras \(K\) and \(K'\) have the same image under the canonical map \(H^1(\mathbb {Q},C_3) \to H^1(\mathbb {Q},S_3)\) because the outer automorphism of \(C_3\) is realized by \(S_3\)-conjugation.
