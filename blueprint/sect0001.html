1 Introduction

We count \(f(t)=t^3+a_1t^2+a_2t+a_3\in \mathbb {Z}[t]\) with Galois group \(C_3\), ordered by \(H(f)=\max (|a_1|,|a_2|^{1/2},|a_3|^{1/3})\). Throughout, \(E=\mathbb {Q}(\sqrt{-3})\) and \(\xi =\zeta _E\). The constants in the main terms are defined first. We write \(S=\mathbb {P}^2/C_3\), \(\mathscr {S}\) for its integral model, \(D_0\) for the image of \(X+Y+Z=0\), and \(T\) for its open torus. The integral structure and toric height are described in the chapters below.

Theorem 1 All monic cubics
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.Counting.GeneralMonic.GeneralMonicCountClaim
  • Cubic.Counting.GeneralMonicCount.«thm:generalMonicCount»

For every \(\varepsilon {\gt}0\), as \(H\to \infty \), the number of monic integral cubic polynomials with Galois group \(C_3\) and root height at most \(H\) is

\[ H^3P(\log H)+O_\varepsilon (H^{2+\varepsilon }), \]

where

\begin{equation*} P(X)=\frac{220\alpha }{243} \biggl(X+\mu -\frac{\zeta '(2)}{\zeta (2)}+ \beta +\log 2-\frac{5}{88}\log 3 +\frac{\pi \sqrt3}{10}-\frac{17}{15}\biggr) -\frac{20\pi \sqrt3}{81}. \end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

Reduce each trace class to trace zero or one. Substitute the asymptotic formulas for \(A_0\) and \(A_1\), approximate their main-term sums by integrals, and then sum the errors. The two real integrals give the displayed polynomial \(P\).

The proof assumes Poisson summation (Equation 15) and the Chandrasekharan–Narasimhan estimate (Cited theorem 2).

Theorem 2 Trace-one cubics
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.Counting.AsymptoticPolynomialCount.Claim
  • Cubic.Counting.AsymptoticPolynomialCount.«thm:asymptoticPolynomialCount»

For every \(\varepsilon {\gt}0\), as \(H\to \infty \), the number of monic integral trace-one cubic polynomials with Galois group \(C_3\) and root height at most \(H\) is

\[ H^2P_1(\log H)+O_\varepsilon (H^{1+\varepsilon }) \]

where

\begin{equation*} P_1(X)=\frac\alpha 3 \biggl(X+\mu -\frac{\zeta '(2)}{\zeta (2)} + \beta +\frac78\log 3-\frac12\biggr) -\frac{\pi }{3\sqrt3}. \end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

Estimate \(\sum _{n\leq X}d_n\) by truncated Perron’s formula and contour shifting. Subtract the split contribution and set \(X=1+3H^2\). The paper uses Alberts’s theorem for the coefficient estimate; Lean retains the direct proof.

The proof assumes Poisson summation (Equation 15) and the Chandrasekharan–Narasimhan estimate (Cited theorem 2).

Theorem 3 An exact count
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.Counting.ExactFormula.Claim
  • Cubic.Counting.ExactFormula.«thm:exactFormula»

For any \(H \geq 1\) let \(E_H \subset \mathbb {R}^2\) be the ellipse defined by \(E_{H} : x^2+y^2+xy-x-y = \tfrac {1}{3}(H^2-1)\). If \(t^3-t^2+at+b\in \mathbb {Z}[t]\) has Galois group \(C_3\), then \(a\leq 0\). Fix \(a \in \mathbb {Z}_{\leq 0}\). The number of polynomials of the form \(t^3 -t^2 + at + b \in \mathbb {Z}[t]\) with Galois group \(C_3\) is equal to

\begin{equation*} \frac12\sum _{d|(1-3a)} 3^{\omega (Q_1(d))} (-1)^{\Omega (Q_2(d))} -\frac16 \# E_{\sqrt{1-3a}}(\mathbb {Z}) \end{equation*}

where \(Q_j(d)\) denotes the largest divisor of \(d\) only divisible by primes \(\equiv j \pmod3\), and \(\omega (n)\) (resp. \(\Omega (n)\)) denotes the number of prime factors of a positive integer \(n\) counted without (resp. with) multiplicity.

Proof in Lean ▼

For \(n=1-3a\), the torus points are counted by \(d_n\). The split points are the free cyclic orbits of \(E_{\sqrt n}(\mathbb {Z})\), and each irreducible polynomial contributes two points. Thus the answer is \(d_n/2-\# E_{\sqrt n}(\mathbb {Z})/6\).

The proof assumes Poisson summation (Equation 15).

Theorem 4 The one-variable height zeta function
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.PoissonSummation.HeightZetaCountingFormula.Claim
  • Cubic.PoissonSummation.«thm:heightZetaFunctionFormula»
\begin{equation*} \sum _{P \in (\mathscr {S}\setminus \mathscr {D}_0)(\mathbb {Z})} H(P)^{-s} = \left(1-\frac{1}{3^{s/2}}\right)^2 \xi \left(\frac s2\right)^2 \prod _{q\equiv 2\pmod{3}}\left(1-\frac{1}{q^{s}}\right) \prod _{p\equiv 1\pmod{3}}\left(1-\frac{3}{p^{s}}+\frac{2}{p^{3s/2}}\right) \end{equation*}

where \(\xi \) is the Dedekind zeta function of \(E\). This height zeta function can be meromorphically continued to the half-plane \(\mathrm{Re}(s){\gt}1\) and its only pole in this region is at \(s = 2\) with order \(2\).

Proof in Lean ▼

Restrict the multivariate formula to the height under consideration and identify its Euler factors with those of \(\xi \). The correction product is holomorphic in the required half-plane; the Laurent expansion gives the unique double pole.

The proof assumes Poisson summation (Equation 15).

Remark 1 The alternative ideal count
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.Introduction.IdealRemark.Claim
  • Cubic.Introduction.IdealRemark.«rem:idealParametrization»

We prompted GPT-\(6.1\) with the contents of an earlier version of this paper and asked it to identify the Dirichlet series in Theorem 4. Using our Eisenstein parametrization (§4) it proved that

\[ \sum _{P \in (\mathscr {S}\setminus \mathscr {D}_0)(\mathbb {Z})} H(P)^{-s} = \sum _{\substack {0\ne J\subseteq \mathcal{O}_E\\ J+3\mathcal{O}_E=\mathcal{O}_E\\ \mathrm N J\text{ a cube}}} N^E_\mathbb {Q}(J)^{-s/6} \]

where \(J \subset \mathcal{O}_E\) runs over integral nonzero ideals. With the help of this description using ideals, we found another result in the literature [ 21 ] that we previously missed. After translating the \(C_3\)-polynomial counting problem into an integral Diophantine problem on \(S^{(-3)}\) (explained in the next section), Kühleitner–Nowak’s  [ 21 , Corollary 2 ] can be used to obtain an alternative proof of Corollary 1 and Theorem 2 without using toric harmonic analysis, and with an error term \(O\bigl(H(\log H)^3(\log \log H)^2\bigr)\). We did not incorporate this argument as the toric approach of this paper has the merit of generalizing readily to other abelian groups where there is no analogue of [ 21 ] .

Proof in Lean ▼

The Eisenstein parametrization identifies integral torus points with ideals of cube norm prime to \(3\). Apply the cube-ideal summatory estimate, remove the Euler factor at \(3\), and compare the constants and heights.

The proof assumes the Kühleitner–Nowak estimate (Cited theorem 3).
