4 The Eisenstein parametrization

Write \(E=\mathbb {Q}(\zeta )\), where \(\zeta \) is a primitive cube root of unity. The isomorphism \(T\cong \operatorname {Res}_{E/\mathbb {Q}}\mathbb {G}_m\) turns rational torus points into elements \(u+v\zeta \in E^\times \). Their norm is \(u^2+v^2-uv\), and their trace is \(2u-v\).

Let \(\gamma \) generate \(\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb {Q})\), and let \(\mathcal G_1\cong R^E_\mathbb {Q}\mathbb {G}_m\) be the torus of units of augmentation one in the group algebra of \(C_3\), so that \(T=\mathcal G_1/C_3\). The cocharacter lattice of \(\mathcal G_{1,E}\) is \(N_E'=\mathbb {Z}\langle v_1,v_2\rangle \), and \(\gamma \) swaps \(v_1\) and \(v_2\). Put \(\omega =\tfrac 13(2v_1+v_2)\); the cocharacter lattice of \(T_E\) is \(N_E=N_E'+\mathbb {Z}\omega \). The following \(\Gamma \)-equivariant isomorphism sends \(\omega \) to \(v_1\) and \(\gamma \omega \) to \(v_2\).

\begin{equation*} N_E=\mathbb {Z}\langle \omega ,\gamma \omega \rangle \to N_E’=\mathbb {Z}\langle v_1,v_2\rangle \end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

We have \(\gamma \omega =\tfrac 13(v_1+2v_2)=v_1+v_2-\omega \in N_E\), and \(v_1=2\omega -\gamma \omega \), \(v_2=-\omega +2\gamma \omega \). So \((\omega ,\gamma \omega )\) is a basis of \(N_E\), and the map sending it to the basis \((v_1,v_2)\) of \(N_E'\) is an isomorphism of lattices. It commutes with \(\gamma \), which swaps \(\omega \) and \(\gamma \omega \) and swaps \(v_1\) and \(v_2\).

The tori \(T\) and \(\mathcal G_1 = R^E_\mathbb {Q}\mathbb {G}_m\) are isomorphic as algebraic groups over \(\mathbb {Q}\). Every rational point \((K/\mathbb {Q},x)\) of \(T\) thereby determines an element of \(\mathbb {Q}(\sqrt{-3})^\times \) which is canonically determined up to the action of \(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal G_1)\). For integral rational points of \(T\), the toric height \(H_{\mathrm{tor}}(f)\mathrel {:=}\sqrt{1-3a}\) is identified with the square-root of the norm on \(\mathbb {Q}(\sqrt{-3})^\times \). Let \(\zeta \) be a primitive cube root of unity. If \(u+v\zeta \in \mathbb {Q}(\sqrt{-3})^\times \) has norm \(N\) and trace \(T\), then the characteristic polynomial of the corresponding rational point \((K/\mathbb {Q},x)\) is

\begin{equation*} f=t^3-t^2+\tfrac {1}{3}(1 - N)t+\tfrac {1}{27}(N(3-T)-1) \in \mathbb {Q}[t]. \end{equation*}

Such a polynomial either has Galois group \(C_3\) or splits into three linear factors over \(\mathbb {Q}\), with at most two linear factors being the same. Conversely, a monic trace-one polynomial \(f=t^3-t^2+at+b \in \mathbb {Q}[t]\) which either has Galois group \(C_3\) or splits into three linear factors over \(\mathbb {Q}\), with at most two linear factors being the same, can be expressed in this way for precisely two rational points of \(T\) if \(f\) has no repeated roots, or for precisely one rational point of \(T\) if \(f\) has a double root which is not a triple root. The elements \(u+v\zeta \in \mathbb {Q}(\sqrt{-3})^\times \) corresponding to \(f=t^3-t^2+at+b\) will be the roots of the quadratic polynomial

\begin{equation*} g = t^2 - \left(3-\frac{1+27b}{1-3a}\right)t + 1-3a \in \mathbb {Q}[t]. \end{equation*}

The polynomial \(f\) will have integral coefficients if and only if

\begin{equation*} \begin{cases} u^2+v^2-uv \in 1+3 \mathbb {Z}\text{ and }\\ (u^2+v^2-uv)(3-2u+v) \in 1+27 \mathbb {Z}. \end{cases}\end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

A torus over \(\mathbb {Q}\) is determined up to isomorphism by its character lattice with the Galois action [ 23 , Theorem 12.23 ] , so the \(\Gamma \)-isomorphism of Equation 13 gives \(T\cong \mathcal G_1\), unique up to \(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal G_1)\). On characters it sends \(\chi ^{v_1^\vee },\chi ^{v_2^\vee }\) of \(\mathcal G_{1,E}\) to \(\chi ^{a'},\chi ^{b'}\) of \(T_E\), and at \(\alpha \in E^\times =\mathcal G_1(\mathbb {Q})\) the characters \(\chi ^{v_1^\vee },\chi ^{v_2^\vee }\) take the values \(\alpha \) and \(\gamma \alpha \) in some order. On the chart \(e_1=1\), Equations 7 and 8 give \(\chi ^{a'+b'}=1-3e_2\) and \(\chi ^{2a'+b'}+\chi ^{a'+2b'}=2-9e_2+27e_3\). At the point given by \(\alpha \), where \(e_2=a\) and \(e_3=-b\), these become \(N=\alpha \cdot \gamma \alpha =1-3a\) and \(NT=N(\alpha +\gamma \alpha )=2-9a-27b\); solving for \(a,b\) gives the formula for \(f\). At integral points Lemma 3 gives \(H_{\mathrm{tor}}(f)^2=1-3a=N\). Since \(N\neq 0\), \(T=3-(1+27b)/N\), so every \(\alpha \) giving \(f\) is a root of \(g=t^2-Tt+N\). The polynomial \(f\) has Galois group \(C_3\) or splits, without a triple root, because \(K\) is a cyclic cubic field or \(\mathbb {Q}^3\) and \(x\) is normal (Theorem 5). For the converse, the roots \(\theta _i\) of \(f\) are real and not all equal, so \(N=1-3a=\tfrac 12\sum _{i{\lt}j}(\theta _i-\theta _j)^2{\gt}0\). Write \(\mathrm{disc}(f)=r^2\) with \(r\in \mathbb {Q}\). Then \(T^2-4N=-27\, \mathrm{disc}(f)/N^2=-3(3r/N)^2\), so the roots \(\alpha _\pm =\tfrac 12\bigl(T\pm \tfrac {3r}{N}\sqrt{-3}\bigr)\) of \(g\) lie in \(E^\times \), are swapped by \(\gamma \), and have trace \(T\) and norm \(N\). By the coefficient formula they give points with characteristic polynomial \(f\), and they are distinct exactly when \(r\neq 0\); this gives two points for separable \(f\) and one when \(f\) has a double root (Supporting lemma 2). Finally, \(u+v\zeta \) has norm \(u^2+v^2-uv\) and trace \(2u-v\), and substituting into \(a=(1-N)/3\) and \(b=(N(3-T)-1)/27\) gives Equation 14.

Let \(u+v\zeta \in E^\times \) correspond, as in Proposition 3, to a rational point of \(T\) with characteristic polynomial \(f=t^3-t^2+at+b\). Then \(f\in \mathbb {Z}[t]\) if and only if the following holds.

\begin{equation*} \begin{cases} u^2+v^2-uv \in 1+3 \mathbb {Z}\text{ and }\\ (u^2+v^2-uv)(3-2u+v) \in 1+27 \mathbb {Z}. \end{cases}\end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

By Proposition 3, \(a=(1-N)/3\) and \(b=(N(3-T)-1)/27\), where \(N=(u+v\zeta )(u+v\zeta ^2)=u^2+v^2-uv\) and \(T=2u-v\). So \(a\in \mathbb {Z}\) exactly when \(N\in 1+3\mathbb {Z}\), and \(b\in \mathbb {Z}\) exactly when \(N(3-2u+v)\in 1+27\mathbb {Z}\) (Supporting lemma 10).
