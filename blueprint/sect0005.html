6 The trace-one counts

Let \(F=F_1\) consist of the monic integral trace-one cubics with Galois group \(C_3\). The coefficients \(d_n\) are those of \(Z_0(z)=\sum _{n\geq 1}d_n n^{-z}\). First we remove the split polynomials from the torus-point count. We then estimate the sum of the coefficients and change from toric height to root height. The polynomial \(Q\) is the one in Corollary 1.

Lemma 9 Normality
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.Counting.NormalityCriterion.Claim
  • Cubic.Counting.NormalityCriterion.«lemma:normalityCriterion»

Let \(K/\mathbb {Q}\) be a \(C_3\)-algebra and let \(x\in K\) have characteristic polynomial \(t^3-e_1t^2+e_2t-e_3\). Then \(x\) is normal if and only if \(e_1(e_1^2-3e_2)\neq 0\).

Proof in Lean ▼

The Gram determinant of the trace pairing on the conjugates of \(x\) is the square of the group determinant. Use its factorization \(\Delta =e_1(e_1^2-3e_2)\) and nondegeneracy of the trace form.

Lemma 10 Removing split polynomials
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.Counting.Reducibles.Claim
  • Cubic.Counting.Reducibles.«lemma:reducibles»

Let \(d_n\) be the coefficients of \(Z_0(z)=\sum _{n\geq 1}d_n n^{-z}\). For \(X\geq 1\),

\begin{equation*} \sum _{\substack {f\in F\\ \begin{bgroup} H_{\mathrm{tor}}(f)\leq \sqrt X \end{bgroup}}}1 =\frac12\sum _{n\leq X}d_n -\frac{\pi }{9\sqrt3}X+O(\sqrt X). \end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

Use the multiplicities of the characteristic-polynomial map. Count the split points by cyclic orbits of integer triples and compare the corresponding ellipse lattice count with its area.

Lemma 11 A uniform mean-square estimate
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.Counting.XiMeanValue.Claim
  • Cubic.Counting.XiMeanValue.«lemma:xiMeanValue»

Let \(\eta {\gt}0\). For \(1/2\leq \sigma \leq 2\) and \(U\geq 2\),

\begin{equation*} \int _U^{2U}|\xi (\sigma +it)|^2\, dt \ll _\eta U^{1+\eta }. \end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

Apply the residue theorem to an exponentially decaying kernel. A reflected kernel keeps the estimate uniform as \(\sigma \) approaches \(1/2\). Integrate over the height interval and apply the critical-line mean-square bound. The paper instead uses Titchmarsh, [ 30 , § 7.8, (7.8.3) ] .

The proof assumes the Chandrasekharan–Narasimhan estimate (Cited theorem 2).

Equation 33 The coefficient sum
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.Counting.GeneralMonic.DSummatoryClaim
  • Cubic.Counting.DSummatory.«eqn:dSummatoryForGeneralTrace»

For every \(\varepsilon {\gt}0\), the following asymptotic holds as \(X\to \infty \).

\begin{equation*} \sum _{n\leq X}d_n =XQ\left(\tfrac 12\log X\right) +O_\varepsilon (X^{1/2+\varepsilon }), \end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

Apply truncated Perron at a half-integer, move the contour across the double pole at \(1\), and estimate the new contour using the strip mean-square bound. The residue supplies the main term. This is the direct argument retained in Lean in place of [ 1 , Theorem 1.1 ] .

The proof assumes Poisson summation (Equation 15) and the Chandrasekharan–Narasimhan estimate (Cited theorem 2).
