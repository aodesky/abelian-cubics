5 Poisson summation

For \(s=(s_0,s_1,s_2)\) invariant under Galois, let \(H(P,s)\) be the toric height and \(Z(s)\) the sum of \(H(P,s)^{-1}\) over integral rational torus points. The function \(1^{D_0}\) imposes integrality at the finite places. Fourier transforms use the Haar measures of the paper, and \(e(t)=\exp (2\pi it)\). The computation starts with the local factors and then applies Poisson summation. Later, \(Z_0(z)=Z(2z,0,0)=\sum _{n\geq 1}d_n n^{-z}\) and \(R(z)\) denotes the correction Euler product. In the spectral formulas write \(s=(s_0,\sigma ,\sigma )\), \(t=m(v_0)\), and \(\lambda =\sigma -\pi it\).

Theorem 6 The multivariate height zeta function
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.PoissonSummation.HeightZetaFunctionFormula.Claim
  • Cubic.PoissonSummation.«thm:heightZetaFunctionFormula2»

Fix any \(s \in (\mathbb {C}^{\Sigma (1)})^{\Gamma }\) with \(\mathrm{Re}(s_0+s_1+s_2) {\gt} 2\). Then the multivariate Dirichlet series

\begin{equation*} Z(s)= \sum _{\substack {P \in T(\mathbb Q)\\ \begin{bgroup} \text{\emph{integral}} \end{bgroup}}} H(P,s)^{-1} \end{equation*}

is absolutely convergent and equals

\begin{align*} \left({1-3^{-z}}\right) \zeta (z) \prod _{q\equiv 2\pmod{3}} \left(1+\frac{1}{q^{z}}\right)^{-1} \prod _{p\equiv 1\pmod{3}} \left(1+\frac{3}{p^z}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^z}\right)^{-1}\right) \end{align*}

where \(z = \frac{1}{2}(s_0+s_1+s_2)\). This multivariate Dirichlet series admits a meromorphic continuation to \(\{ s \in (\mathbb {C}^{\Sigma (1)})^{\Gamma } : \mathrm{Re}(s_0+s_1+s_2) {\gt} 1\} \).

Proof in Lean ▼

Compute the local Fourier transforms, apply Poisson summation, and evaluate the resulting real integral by residues. Shift the height parameters to obtain the full convergence region, then identify the continued Euler product.

The proof assumes Poisson summation (Equation 15).

Proposition 4 The local integral condition
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.PoissonSummation.LocalIntegrality.Claim
  • Cubic.PoissonSummation.LocalIntegrality.«prop:localIntegrality»

Let \(v\) be a finite place of \(\mathbb {Q}\) and let \(w\) be a place of \(E\) lying over \(v\).

  1. If \(v \neq 3\), then \(1_v^{D_0}(x) = 1\) if and only if \(\ell _w(x) \in \mathbb R_{\geq 0}v_1+\mathbb R_{\geq 0}v_2\). In particular \(K_v^{D_0}=K_v^{\mathrm{max}}\).

  2. Let \(v = 3\), let \(w\) be the unique place of \(E\) over \(3\), and let \(\mathfrak w = (1-\zeta )O_{E,w}\) be the maximal ideal of \(O_{E,w}\), so that \(3O_{E,w} = \mathfrak w^2\). The support of \(1_3^{D_0}\) is the subgroup

    \begin{equation*} K_{3,2}\mathrel {:=}\iota ^{-1}(1+\mathfrak w^2) =\iota ^{-1}(1+3O_{E,w})\subset K_3^{\mathrm{max}}. \end{equation*}

  3. Let \(K^{\mathrm{max}}=\prod _v K_v^{\mathrm{max}}\) and \(K^{D_0}=\prod _v K_v^{D_0}\subset K^{\mathrm{max}}\). The latter is the subgroup which fixes the characteristic function \(1^{D_0}=\otimes _v1_v^{D_0}\) for integral points in \(T(\mathbb {A})\), with \(1_\infty ^{D_0}=1\). Then

    \[ K_3^{D_0}=K_{3,2},\qquad K^{D_0}=K_{3,2}\times \prod _{v\neq 3}K_v^{\mathrm{max}}. \]
Proof in Lean ▼

Away from \(3\), evaluate the invariant functions in local coordinates to obtain the cone condition. At \(3\), the same computation gives the subgroup \(1+3O_{E,w}\). Its stabilizer determines the global compact level.

Proposition 5 Local Fourier transforms
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.PoissonSummation.HeightFourierTransform.Claim
  • Cubic.PoissonSummation.HeightFourierTransform.«prop:heightFourierTransform»

Let \(s \in (\mathbb {C}^{\Sigma (1)})^{\Gamma }\) and assume \(\mathrm{Re}(s_e){\gt}0\) for each \(e \in \{ 0,1,2\} \). Let \(w\) be the infinite place of \(E\). Let \(\chi \in T(\mathbb {R})^\vee \) be a unitary character. If \(\chi \) is ramified then \(\widehat{H_\infty }(\chi ,-s,D_0)\) is identically zero. If \(\chi \) is unramified, then \(\chi (x)= e(\langle \ell _w(x) ,m\rangle )\) for all \(x \in T(\mathbb {R})\) for a unique \(m \in \mathfrak t^\vee \), and

\begin{equation*} \widehat{H_\infty }(\chi ,-s,D_0) = \left(\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\right) \frac{s_0+s_1+s_2}{2\pi i} \frac{1}{(m(v_0)+\frac{s_0}{2\pi i})(m(v_0)-\frac{s_1+s_2}{2\pi i})}. \end{equation*}

Next let \(v\) be a finite place of \(\mathbb {Q}\). For any unitary character \(\chi \in T(\mathbb {Q}_v)^\vee \), the integral defining \(\widehat{H_v}(\chi ,-s,D_0)\) converges absolutely to a holomorphic function of \(s\) in the region

\[ \{ s \in (\mathbb {C}^{\Sigma (1)})^\Gamma : \mathrm{Re}(s_1),\mathrm{Re}(s_2){\gt}0 \} . \]

Assume \(v \neq 3\). Let \(w\) be any place of \(E\) lying over \(v\). The local characteristic function \(1_v^{D_0}\) is \(K_v^{\mathrm{max}}\)-invariant. If \(\chi \) is ramified, then \(\widehat{H_v}(\chi ,-s,D_0)\) is identically zero. If \(\chi \) is unramified then we may regard \(\chi \) as a character on \(X_\ast (T_{E})^{\Gamma (w/v)}\) (Proposition 1) and

\begin{equation*} \widehat{H_v}(\chi ,-s,D_0) = \sum _{\substack {n \in X_\ast (T_{E})^{\Gamma (w/v)}\\ \begin{bgroup} n \in \mathbb R_{\geq 0}v_1+\mathbb R_{\geq 0}v_2 \end{bgroup}} } \chi (n)^{-1} q_v^{\varphi (n)} . \end{equation*}

If \(v = 3\) then the support of \(x \mapsto H_3(x,-s,D_0)\) is the congruence subgroup \(K_{3,2}\) of \(K_3^{\mathrm{max}}\) of index six. Under the isomorphism \(\iota :T(\mathbb {Q}_3)\to E_w^\times \) the support corresponds to the subgroup \(1+3O_{E,w}\) of \(O_{E,w}^\times \) where \(w\) is the unique place of \(E\) lying over \(3\).

Proof in Lean ▼

At infinity and at finite places other than \(3\), ramified characters vanish after integration over the maximal compact subgroup. For unramified characters, integrate the archimedean support function on its two cones and sum the finite valuation lattices. At \(3\), integrate over the congruence subgroup of index six.

Remark 2 Why the parameters are kept separate
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.PoissonSummation.LocalNonintegrability.Claim
  • Cubic.PoissonSummation.LocalNonintegrability.«rem:localNonintegrability»

The local Fourier transforms — and therefore the entire Poisson summation argument — must be computed before restricting to the line in \(\mathrm{Pic}^T(S) \otimes \mathbb {C}\) spanned by the \(T_E\)-line bundle \(\mathcal O(D_0)\) of interest since \(x \mapsto H_v(x,-s,D_0)\) will not be integrable for any place \(v \neq 3,\infty \) once either of \(s_1\) or \(s_2\) vanishes, no matter how large and positive \(\mathrm{Re}(s_0)\) is.

Proof in Lean ▼

Along a boundary ray where a height exponent vanishes, the local valuation series has terms of constant absolute value. It therefore fails to converge.

Proposition 6 The adelic logarithm
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.PoissonSummation.LogarithmExactSequence.Claim
  • Cubic.PoissonSummation.«prop:Lmap»

There is an exact sequence

\begin{equation*} 1 \longrightarrow K^{\mathrm{max}}/\mu ^{\mathrm{max}} \longrightarrow T(\mathbb Q)\backslash T(\mathbb {A}) \xrightarrow {\ell } \mathfrak t \longrightarrow 0 \end{equation*}

where \(K^{\mathrm{max}}\) is the maximal compact subgroup of \(T(\mathbb {A})\) and \(\mu ^{\mathrm{max}} = T(\mathbb {Q}) \cap K^{\mathrm{max}}\).

Proof in Lean ▼

The product formula makes the logarithm descend to the adelic quotient. Principalize the finite valuations to identify its kernel with the compact quotient; the infinite component supplies surjectivity.

Lemma 4 The quotient by the integral level
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.PoissonSummation.KprimeGroup.Claim
  • Cubic.PoissonSummation.«lemma:KprimeGroup»

Let \(K^{D_0}=K_{3,2}\times \prod _{v\neq 3}K_v^{\mathrm{max}}\) be the subgroup of \(K^{\mathrm{max}}\) fixing \(1^{D_0}\), as in Proposition 4. The map \(\ell \) induces an isomorphism

\begin{equation*} T(\mathbb {Q})\backslash T(\mathbb {A})/K^{D_0} \xrightarrow {\sim } \mathfrak t. \end{equation*}

Let \(a(u)\in T(\mathbb {A})\) have infinite component \(\iota ^{-1}(e^u)\) and all finite components one, so \(\ell (a(u))=uv_0\). In this coordinate, the pushforward of the quotient measure is \(du/6\).

Proof in Lean ▼

The six Eisenstein units represent the residue classes at the ramified place. They identify the quotient by the integral level with the logarithm line. Tracking the normalized Haar measure gives \(du/6\).

Lemma 5 The finite Fourier product
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.PoissonSummation.AdelicFiniteFourierProduct.Claim
  • Cubic.PoissonSummation.«lemma:finiteFourierProduct»

For \(\operatorname {Re}(\sigma ){\gt}1\) and \(t\in \mathbb {R}\), put \(\lambda =\sigma -\pi it\). Then

\begin{equation*} \widehat{H_{\mathrm{fin}}}(\chi _m,-s,D_0) =\frac16 \prod _{q\equiv 2\pmod3}(1-q^{-2\lambda })^{-1} \prod _{p\equiv 1\pmod3} \frac{1+2p^{-\lambda }}{(1-p^{-\lambda })^2} \end{equation*}

and

\begin{equation*} \left|\widehat{H_{\mathrm{fin}}}(\chi _m,-s,D_0)\right| \leq \widehat{H_{\mathrm{fin}}}(\chi _0,{-\operatorname {Re}(s)},D_0){\lt}\infty . \end{equation*}

Here \(\chi _0\) is the trivial character, corresponding to \(m=0\). The Euler product converges absolutely and locally uniformly for \(\operatorname {Re}(\lambda ){\gt}1\) and is bounded on every half-plane \(\operatorname {Re}(\lambda )\geq \tau {\gt}1\).

Proof in Lean ▼

Sum the local valuation series separately at split and inert primes and include the factor \(1/6\) at \(3\). A convergent positive Euler product bounds the absolute values and proves locally uniform convergence.

Remark 3 The coefficients
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.PoissonSummation.HeightCoefficients.Claim
  • Cubic.PoissonSummation.HeightCoefficients.«rem:heightCoefficients»

Comparing Euler factors in 27 gives the coefficients explicitly: \(d_n=0\) if \(3\mid n\), and if \(3\nmid n\) then

\[ d_n=\sum _{d\mid n}3^{\omega (d_1)}(-1)^{\Omega (d_2)}, \]

where \(d=d_1d_2\) with \(d_1\) divisible only by primes \(p\equiv 1\pmod3\) and \(d_2\) only by primes \(q\equiv 2\pmod3\). Equivalently, \(d_n\) is the product of \(3k+1\) over prime powers \(p^k\parallel n\) with \(p\equiv 1\pmod3\) if every prime \(q\equiv 2\pmod3\) divides \(n\) to an even power, and \(d_n=0\) otherwise.

Proof in Lean ▼

Expand the split, inert, and ramified Euler factors and multiply the prime-power coefficients.

The proof assumes Poisson summation (Equation 15).

Proposition 7 Laurent coefficients
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.PoissonSummation.LeadingTermsNormalized.Claim
  • Cubic.PoissonSummation.«prop:leadingTerms»

The only pole of \(Z_0(z)\) in the half-plane \(\mathrm{Re}(z){\gt}1/2\) is a double pole at \(z=1\). Its Laurent expansion there is

\begin{equation*} Z_0(z)=\frac{\alpha }{9(z-1)^2} +\frac{2\alpha }{9}\left(\mu -\frac{\zeta '(2)}{\zeta (2)}+\beta +\frac38\log 3\right)\frac{1}{z-1}+O(1), \end{equation*}

where \(\alpha \) and \(\beta \) are as in 1.

Proof in Lean ▼

Expand the Dedekind zeta factor at \(1\) and logarithmically differentiate the convergent correction product. Simplifying the prime factors yields \(\alpha \), \(\beta \), and the Euler–Kronecker constant \(\mu \).

The proof assumes Poisson summation (Equation 15).
