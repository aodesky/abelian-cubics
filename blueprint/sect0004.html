5 Poisson summation

For \(s=(s_0,s_1,s_2)\) invariant under Galois, let \(H(P,s)\) be the toric height and \(Z(s)\) the sum of \(H(P,s)^{-1}\) over integral rational torus points. The function \(1^{D_0}\) imposes integrality at the finite places. Fourier transforms use the Haar measures of the paper, and \(e(t)=\exp (2\pi it)\). The computation starts with the local factors and then applies Poisson summation. Later, \(Z_0(z)=Z(2z,0,0)=\sum _{n\geq 1}d_n n^{-z}\) and \(R(z)\) denotes the correction Euler product. In the spectral formulas write \(s=(s_0,\sigma ,\sigma )\), \(t=m(v_0)\), and \(\lambda =\sigma -\pi it\).

Fix any \(s \in (\mathbb {C}^{\Sigma (1)})^{\Gamma }\) with \(\mathrm{Re}(s_0+s_1+s_2) {\gt} 2\). Then the multivariate Dirichlet series

\begin{equation*} Z(s)= \sum _{\substack {P \in T(\mathbb Q)\\ { \text{\emph{integral}} }}} H(P,s)^{-1} \end{equation*}

is absolutely convergent and equals

\begin{align*} \left({1-3^{-z}}\right) \zeta (z) \prod _{q\equiv 2\pmod{3}} \left(1+\frac{1}{q^{z}}\right)^{-1} \prod _{p\equiv 1\pmod{3}} \left(1+\frac{3}{p^z}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^z}\right)^{-1}\right) \end{align*}

where \(z = \frac{1}{2}(s_0+s_1+s_2)\). This multivariate Dirichlet series admits a meromorphic continuation to \(\{ s \in (\mathbb {C}^{\Sigma (1)})^{\Gamma } : \mathrm{Re}(s_0+s_1+s_2) {\gt} 1\} \).

Proof in Lean ▼

Write \(s_1=s_2=\sigma \) and \(z=s_0/2+\sigma \), and first assume \(\operatorname {Re}(s_0){\gt}0\) and \(\operatorname {Re}(\sigma ){\gt}1\). Lemma 7 writes \(Z(s)\) as \(6\int _\mathbb {R}\) of the product of the archimedean factor 23 and the finite Euler product 21, as functions of \(t\). For \(\operatorname {Im}(t)\geq 0\) we have \(\operatorname {Re}(\lambda )\geq \operatorname {Re}(\sigma ){\gt}1\), so the finite factor is holomorphic and bounded in the upper half-plane (Lemma 5), while the archimedean factor is \(O(|t|^{-2})\). Closing the contour upwards picks up only the pole \(t_0=-s_0/(2\pi i)\) of the archimedean factor, with residue \(1/(2\pi i)\); there \(\lambda =z\), so \(Z(s)\) is \(6\) times the finite product at \(\lambda =z\), namely \(\prod _{q\equiv 2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3)}(1-q^{-2z})^{-1}\prod _{p\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3)}(1+2p^{-z})(1-p^{-z})^{-2}\). Pulling out a factor \((1-p^{-z})^{-1}\) at every prime \(p\neq 3\) gives the stated product. To reach every \(z\) with \(\operatorname {Re}(z){\gt}1\), note that by the product formula \(H(P,s)\) for \(P\in T(\mathbb {Q})\) depends only on \(s\) modulo complex multiples of principal toric divisors, and \(-2D_0+D_1+D_2\) is principal (Equation 25). So \(Z(s)\) depends only on \(z\), and the representative \(s_0=z-1\), \(\sigma =(z+1)/2\) satisfies the initial hypotheses. The meromorphic continuation comes from Lemma 8.

The proof assumes Poisson summation (Equation 15).

For a finite place \(v\) of \(\mathbb {Q}\), let \(1_v^{D_0}\) be the indicator function of the set of \(x\in T(\mathbb {Q}_v)\) at which the functions \(e_2\), \(e_3\) and \(\delta \) of Equation 9 take values in \(\mathbb {Z}_v\), and let \(K_v^{D_0}\) be the subgroup of \(K_v^{\mathrm{max}}\) fixing \(1_v^{D_0}\). Write \(\iota :T\to R^E_\mathbb {Q}\mathbb {G}_m\) for the isomorphism induced by Equation 13, and let \(\zeta \in E\) be a primitive cube root of unity.

Let \(v\) be a finite place of \(\mathbb {Q}\) and let \(w\) be a place of \(E\) lying over \(v\).

  1. If \(v \neq 3\), then \(1_v^{D_0}(x) = 1\) if and only if \(\ell _w(x) \in \mathbb R_{\geq 0}v_1+\mathbb R_{\geq 0}v_2\). In particular \(K_v^{D_0}=K_v^{\mathrm{max}}\).

  2. Let \(v = 3\), let \(w\) be the unique place of \(E\) over \(3\), and let \(\mathfrak w = (1-\zeta )O_{E,w}\) be the maximal ideal of \(O_{E,w}\), so that \(3O_{E,w} = \mathfrak w^2\). The support of \(1_3^{D_0}\) is the subgroup

    \begin{equation*} K_{3,2}\mathrel {:=}\iota ^{-1}(1+\mathfrak w^2) =\iota ^{-1}(1+3O_{E,w})\subset K_3^{\mathrm{max}}. \end{equation*}

  3. Let \(K^{\mathrm{max}}=\prod _v K_v^{\mathrm{max}}\) and \(K^{D_0}=\prod _v K_v^{D_0}\subset K^{\mathrm{max}}\). The latter is the subgroup which fixes the characteristic function \(1^{D_0}=\otimes _v1_v^{D_0}\) for integral points in \(T(\mathbb {A})\), with \(1_\infty ^{D_0}=1\). Then

    \[ K_3^{D_0}=K_{3,2},\qquad K^{D_0}=K_{3,2}\times \prod _{v\neq 3}K_v^{\mathrm{max}}. \]
Proof in Lean ▼

Let \(v\neq 3\). Since \(\delta \) is integral over \(\mathbb {Z}[e_2,e_3]\), the argument of Lemma 2 shows \(1_v^{D_0}(x)=1\) exactly when \(e_2(x),e_3(x)\in \mathbb {Z}_v\). Put \(r=\chi ^{v_1^\vee +v_2^\vee }(x)\), \(y_1=\chi ^{3v_1^\vee }(x)\) and \(y_2=\chi ^{3v_2^\vee }(x)\) in \(E_w\); the character formulas of Lemma 1 give \(r=1-3e_2\), \(y_1+y_2=2-9e_2+27e_3\) and \(y_1y_2=r^3\). If \(e_2,e_3\in \mathbb {Z}_v\), then \(y_1,y_2\) are roots of \(U^2-(y_1+y_2)U+r^3\in \mathbb {Z}_v[U]\) and so lie in \(O_{E,w}\). Conversely, if \(y_1,y_2\in O_{E,w}\) then \(r\in O_{E,w}\), and \(e_2=(1-r)/3\), \(e_3=(y_1+y_2+1-3r)/27\) lie in \(O_{E,w}\cap \mathbb {Q}_v=\mathbb {Z}_v\) because \(3\) is a unit. Since \(\mathrm{ord}_w(y_1)=3\langle v_1^\vee ,\ell _w(x)\rangle \) and \(\mathrm{ord}_w(y_2)=3\langle v_2^\vee ,\ell _w(x)\rangle \), this is the cone condition; it depends only on \(\ell _w(x)\), which vanishes on \(K_v^{\mathrm{max}}\) (Proposition 1). At \(v=3\), let \(u=\iota (P)\) have norm \(N\) and trace \(T\); by Equation 14, read over \(\mathbb {Z}_3\), \(P\) is integral exactly when \(N\in 1+3\mathbb {Z}_3\) and \(N(3-T)\in 1+27\mathbb {Z}_3\). If these hold, then \(u\) is a unit, and writing \(N=1+3n\) gives \(T\equiv 2+3n-9n^2\pmod{27}\), so \(\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak w}(T-2)\geq 2\) and \(\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak w}(1-T+N)\geq 4\). The Newton polygon of \((U-1)^2-(T-2)(U-1)+(1-T+N)\), the characteristic polynomial of \(u\), then gives \(u\in 1+\mathfrak w^2\). Conversely, for \(u=1+3x\) with \(x=c+d\zeta \) and \(c,d\in \mathbb {Z}_3\), one computes \(\mathrm N(u)\in 1+3\mathbb {Z}_3\) and \(\mathrm N(u)(3-\mathrm{Tr}(u))=1+9(\mathrm N(x)-\mathrm{Tr}(x)^2)-27\mathrm N(x)\mathrm{Tr}(x)\), which lies in \(1+27\mathbb {Z}_3\) because \(\mathrm{Tr}(x)^2-\mathrm N(x)=3c(c-d)\). Part (3) follows: \(1_3^{D_0}\) is the indicator function of the subgroup \(K_{3,2}\), whose stabilizer in \(K_3^{\mathrm{max}}\) is \(K_{3,2}\) itself.

Give \(T(\mathbb {Q})\) counting measure, \(T(\mathbb {Q})\backslash T(\mathbb {A})\) the quotient of the Haar measure \(d^\times x\), and \(T(\mathbb {Q})^\perp \) the dual measure \(d\chi \), for which Fourier inversion holds on \(T(\mathbb {Q})\backslash T(\mathbb {A})\). Proposition 5 Local Fourier transforms

For a place \(v\) of \(\mathbb {Q}\) put \(H_v(x,-s,D_0)=H_v(x,s)^{-1}1_v^{D_0}(x)\) for \(x\in T(\mathbb {Q}_v)\), where \(H_v(x,s)\) is the local factor of the height in Equation 5 and \(1_\infty ^{D_0}=1\). Its Fourier transform is \(\widehat{H_v}(\chi ,-s,D_0)=\int _{T(\mathbb {Q}_v)}H_v(x,-s,D_0)\chi (x)^{-1}\, d^\times x_v\). At a finite place \(d^\times x_v\) gives \(K_v^{\mathrm{max}}\) measure one; at infinity it is chosen so that \(\mathbb {Z}v_0\) is unimodular for its pushforward to \(\mathfrak t\) under \(\ell _w\). A character of \(T(\mathbb {Q}_v)\) is unramified if it is trivial on \(K_v^{\mathrm{max}}\), and ramified otherwise. The subgroup \(K_{3,2}\) is defined in Proposition 4.

Let \(s \in (\mathbb {C}^{\Sigma (1)})^{\Gamma }\) and assume \(\mathrm{Re}(s_e){\gt}0\) for each \(e \in \{ 0,1,2\} \). Let \(w\) be the infinite place of \(E\). Let \(\chi \in T(\mathbb {R})^\vee \) be a unitary character. If \(\chi \) is ramified then \(\widehat{H_\infty }(\chi ,-s,D_0)\) is identically zero. If \(\chi \) is unramified, then \(\chi (x)= e(\langle \ell _w(x) ,m\rangle )\) for all \(x \in T(\mathbb {R})\) for a unique \(m \in \mathfrak t^\vee \), and

\begin{equation*} \widehat{H_\infty }(\chi ,-s,D_0) = \left(\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\right) \frac{s_0+s_1+s_2}{2\pi i} \frac{1}{(m(v_0)+\frac{s_0}{2\pi i})(m(v_0)-\frac{s_1+s_2}{2\pi i})}. \end{equation*}

Next let \(v\) be a finite place of \(\mathbb {Q}\). For any unitary character \(\chi \in T(\mathbb {Q}_v)^\vee \), the integral defining \(\widehat{H_v}(\chi ,-s,D_0)\) converges absolutely to a holomorphic function of \(s\) in the region

\[ \{ s \in (\mathbb {C}^{\Sigma (1)})^\Gamma : \mathrm{Re}(s_1),\mathrm{Re}(s_2){\gt}0 \} . \]

Assume \(v \neq 3\). Let \(w\) be any place of \(E\) lying over \(v\). The local characteristic function \(1_v^{D_0}\) is \(K_v^{\mathrm{max}}\)-invariant. If \(\chi \) is ramified, then \(\widehat{H_v}(\chi ,-s,D_0)\) is identically zero. If \(\chi \) is unramified then we may regard \(\chi \) as a character on \(X_\ast (T_{E})^{\Gamma (w/v)}\) (Proposition 1) and

\begin{equation*} \widehat{H_v}(\chi ,-s,D_0) = \sum _{\substack {n \in X_\ast (T_{E})^{\Gamma (w/v)}\\ { n \in \mathbb R_{\geq 0}v_1+\mathbb R_{\geq 0}v_2 }} } \chi (n)^{-1} q_v^{\varphi (n)} . \end{equation*}

If \(v = 3\) then the support of \(x \mapsto H_3(x,-s,D_0)\) is the congruence subgroup \(K_{3,2}\) of \(K_3^{\mathrm{max}}\) of index six. Under the isomorphism \(\iota :T(\mathbb {Q}_3)\to E_w^\times \) the support corresponds to the subgroup \(1+3O_{E,w}\) of \(O_{E,w}^\times \) where \(w\) is the unique place of \(E\) lying over \(3\).

Proof in Lean ▼

At infinity \(1_\infty ^{D_0}=1\) and the height is \(K_\infty ^{\mathrm{max}}\)-invariant, so a ramified character integrates to zero over \(K_\infty ^{\mathrm{max}}\). For unramified \(\chi \), push the integral forward by \(\ell _w\) to \(\mathfrak t_w\), where the integrand is \(e^{\varphi (y)}e(-\langle y,m\rangle )\). On the half-lines \(\mathbb {R}_{\geq 0}v_0\) and \(\mathbb {R}_{\geq 0}(-v_0)\) the support function \(\varphi \) has slopes \(-s_0\) and \(-(s_1+s_2)\), since \(-v_0=v_1+v_2\); integrating the two exponentials gives \((s_0+2\pi im(v_0))^{-1}+(s_1+s_2-2\pi im(v_0))^{-1}\), which rearranges to 16. At a finite place \(v\neq 3\), Proposition 4 shows that \(1_v^{D_0}\) is \(K_v^{\mathrm{max}}\)-invariant and is the indicator of the cone \(\mathbb {R}_{\geq 0}v_1+\mathbb {R}_{\geq 0}v_2\) in the \(\ell _w\)-coordinate; the height is also \(K_v^{\mathrm{max}}\)-invariant. So ramified characters give zero, and for unramified \(\chi \) the integral becomes the lattice sum 18 over the image \(X_\ast (T_E)^{\Gamma (w/v)}\) of \(\ell _w\) (Proposition 1). On the cone \(\operatorname {Re}\varphi (n_1v_1+n_2v_2)=-\operatorname {Re}(s_1)n_1-\operatorname {Re}(s_2)n_2\), so the sum converges absolutely and locally uniformly when \(\operatorname {Re}(s_1),\operatorname {Re}(s_2){\gt}0\). At \(v=3\), Proposition 4 identifies the support with \(K_{3,2}\), whose index in \(K_3^{\mathrm{max}}\) is \(\# (O_{E,w}/3O_{E,w})^\times =6\); the local height is one there, so \(\widehat{H_3}(\chi ,-s,D_0)\) is \(1/6\) if \(\chi \) is trivial on \(K_{3,2}\) and \(0\) otherwise.

Remark 2 Why the parameters are kept separate
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.PoissonSummation.LocalNonintegrability.Claim
  • Cubic.PoissonSummation.LocalNonintegrability.«rem:localNonintegrability»

The local Fourier transforms — and therefore the entire Poisson summation argument — must be computed before restricting to the line in \(\mathrm{Pic}^T(S) \otimes \mathbb {C}\) spanned by the \(T_E\)-line bundle \(\mathcal O(D_0)\) of interest since \(x \mapsto H_v(x,-s,D_0)\) will not be integrable for any place \(v \neq 3,\infty \) once either of \(s_1\) or \(s_2\) vanishes, no matter how large and positive \(\mathrm{Re}(s_0)\) is.

Proof in Lean ▼

Since \(s\) is Galois-invariant, \(s_1=s_2\), so if one vanishes both do. Let \(v\neq 3\) be finite. As in 18, the integral of \(|H_v(\cdot ,-s,D_0)|\) over \(T(\mathbb {Q}_v)\) is the sum of \(q_v^{\operatorname {Re}\varphi (n)}\) over the points \(n\) of \(X_\ast (T_E)^{\Gamma (w/v)}\) in the cone \(\mathbb {R}_{\geq 0}v_1+\mathbb {R}_{\geq 0}v_2\). On this cone \(\varphi \) is linear with \(\varphi (v_1)=-s_1\) and \(\varphi (v_2)=-s_2\); it does not involve \(s_0\) and vanishes when \(s_1=s_2=0\). The cone contains the infinitely many invariant lattice points \(k(v_1+v_2)\), \(k\geq 0\), each contributing \(1\), so the integral diverges.

Proposition 6 The adelic logarithm

Here \(\ell \) is the adelic logarithm of Equation 19.

There is an exact sequence

\begin{equation*} 1 \longrightarrow K^{\mathrm{max}}/\mu ^{\mathrm{max}} \longrightarrow T(\mathbb Q)\backslash T(\mathbb {A}) \xrightarrow {\ell } \mathfrak t \longrightarrow 0 \end{equation*}

where \(K^{\mathrm{max}}\) is the maximal compact subgroup of \(T(\mathbb {A})\) and \(\mu ^{\mathrm{max}} = T(\mathbb {Q}) \cap K^{\mathrm{max}}\).

Proof in Lean ▼

Under \(\iota \), the quotient \(T(\mathbb {Q})\backslash T(\mathbb {A})\) is the idèle class group \(E^\times \backslash \mathbb {A}_E^\times \). By Equation 19, \(\ell \) depends only on \(|\mathrm N(x)|_\mathbb {A}\); it is trivial on \(T(\mathbb {Q})\) by the product formula, and its kernel is the group of idèle classes of norm one. Since \(E\) has class number one, every idèle class has a representative that is a unit at every finite place; if the class has norm one, the infinite component also has absolute value one, so the representative lies in \(K^{\mathrm{max}}\). Two elements of \(K^{\mathrm{max}}\) give the same class exactly when they differ by an element of \(T(\mathbb {Q})\cap K^{\mathrm{max}}=\mu ^{\mathrm{max}}\), so the kernel is \(K^{\mathrm{max}}/\mu ^{\mathrm{max}}\). Finally \(\ell \) is surjective because \(\ell _w\) is surjective at the complex place \(w\) of \(E\) (Proposition 1).

Let \(K^{D_0}=K_{3,2}\times \prod _{v\neq 3}K_v^{\mathrm{max}}\) be the subgroup of \(K^{\mathrm{max}}\) fixing \(1^{D_0}\), as in Proposition 4. The map \(\ell \) induces an isomorphism

\begin{equation*} T(\mathbb {Q})\backslash T(\mathbb {A})/K^{D_0} \xrightarrow {\sim } \mathfrak t. \end{equation*}

Let \(a(u)\in T(\mathbb {A})\) have infinite component \(\iota ^{-1}(e^u)\) and all finite components one, so \(\ell (a(u))=uv_0\). In this coordinate, the pushforward of the quotient measure is \(du/6\).

Proof in Lean ▼

The map \(\ell \) vanishes on \(K^{D_0}\), so it descends to the double quotient, and by Proposition 6 the descended map is surjective with kernel \(K^{\mathrm{max}}/(\mu ^{\mathrm{max}}K^{D_0})\). A point of \(T(\mathbb {Q})\) lies in \(K^{\mathrm{max}}\) exactly when its image under \(\iota \) is a unit at every finite place, so \(\iota (\mu ^{\mathrm{max}})=O_E^\times =\{ \pm 1,\pm \zeta ,\pm \zeta ^2\} \), where \(\zeta \) is a primitive cube root of unity. Projecting to the place \(3\) and reducing modulo \(3\) identifies \(K^{\mathrm{max}}/K^{D_0}\) with \(K_3^{\mathrm{max}}/K_{3,2}\cong (O_{E,w}/3O_{E,w})^\times \), a group of order six. No sixth root of unity other than \(1\) is congruent to \(1\) modulo \(3\), so \(\mu ^{\mathrm{max}}\) maps bijectively onto this group. Hence \(K^{\mathrm{max}}=\mu ^{\mathrm{max}}K^{D_0}\), which gives the isomorphism, and \(T(\mathbb {Q})\cap K^{D_0}=\{ 1\} \), so every class has a unique representative in \(a(\mathbb {R})K^{D_0}\). In this representative the infinite factor contributes \(du\) times an angular measure of total mass one, and the finite part of \(K^{D_0}\) has measure \([K_3^{\mathrm{max}}:K_{3,2}]^{-1}=1/6\).
