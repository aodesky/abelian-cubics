3 Toric heights

The height is defined from the fan of \(S\). We retain the paper’s notation: \(v_0,v_1,v_2\) are its primitive rays, \(M_E\) is the character lattice, and \(\ell _w\) is the local logarithm map. At a finite place, this map records the normalized valuations of characters; at the infinite place it records their logarithmic absolute values. The local height is the exponential of the corresponding piecewise linear support function. We write \(e_1=X+Y+Z\), \(e_2=XY+YZ+ZX\), and \(e_3=XYZ\). In the affine chart, \(\varepsilon =e_1=X+Y+Z\).

Let \(\Gamma =\{ 1,\gamma \} \) be the Galois group of \(E=\mathbb {Q}(\zeta )\) over \(\mathbb {Q}\), and for a place \(w\) of \(E\) over \(v\) let \(\Gamma (w/v)\subseteq \Gamma \) be its decomposition group and \(\mathcal{O}_w\) the ring of integers of \(E_w\). Let \(\mathcal G_1\cong R^E_\mathbb {Q}\mathbb {G}_m\) be the torus of units of augmentation one in the group algebra of \(C_3\), so that \(T=\mathcal G_1/C_3\). The characters of \(\mathcal G_{1,E}\) form a lattice \(M_E'\) with basis \((v_1^\vee ,v_2^\vee )\), given by the eigenvalues of multiplication on the two nontrivial idempotents of \(E[C_3]\); \(\gamma \) swaps \(v_1^\vee \) and \(v_2^\vee \). Let \(N_E'\) be the dual lattice, with dual basis \((v_1,v_2)\), and put \(\omega =\tfrac 13(2v_1+v_2)\). The cocharacter lattice of \(T_E\) is \(X_\ast (T_E)=N_E=N_E'+\mathbb {Z}\omega =\mathbb {Z}\langle \omega ,\gamma \omega \rangle \), and its character lattice \(M_E\) is the dual of \(N_E\). Put \(\mathfrak t_E=N_E\otimes \mathbb {R}\) and \(\mathfrak t_w=\mathfrak t_E^{\Gamma (w/v)}\). For \(x\in T(\mathbb {Q}_v)\), \(\ell _w(x)\in \mathfrak t_E\) is the cocharacter \(\chi \mapsto \mathrm{ord}_w(\chi (x))\) on \(\chi \in M_E\), and \(K_v^{\mathrm{max}}\) is the maximal compact subgroup of \(T(\mathbb {Q}_v)\).

Let \(w\) be a place of \(E\) lying over a place \(v\) of \(\mathbb {Q}\). There is an exact sequence

\begin{equation*} 1 \longrightarrow K_v^{\mathrm{max}} \longrightarrow T(\mathbb {Q}_v) \xrightarrow {\ell _w} \mathfrak t_w. \end{equation*}

If \(w\) is infinite then \(\ell _w\) is surjective, and if \(w\) is finite then the image of \(\ell _w\) is the lattice \(X_\ast (T_{E})^{\Gamma (w/v)}\).

Proof in Lean ▼

Evaluating characters identifies \(T(\mathbb {Q}_v)\) with \(\mathrm{Hom}_{\Gamma (w/v)}(M_E,E_w^\times )\). Under this identification \(\ell _w\) is composition with \(\mathrm{ord}_w\), with values in \(\mathrm{Hom}_{\Gamma (w/v)}(M_E,\mathbb {Z})=X_\ast (T_E)^{\Gamma (w/v)}\), and for finite \(v\) its kernel \(\mathrm{Hom}_{\Gamma (w/v)}(M_E,\mathcal{O}_w^\times )\) is \(K_v^{\mathrm{max}}\). By [ 14 , (1.3), p. 449 ] this gives the exact sequence and the asserted image, except that at the ramified place \(w\) over \(3\) it only shows that the image has finite index. At that place we pass to \(\mathcal G_1\). The transpose of the \(\Gamma \)-isomorphism \(N_E\to N_E'\) of Equation 13 gives a commutative diagram identifying the sequence \(\mathrm{Hom}_{\Gamma (w/v)}(M_E',\mathcal{O}_w^\times )\to \mathrm{Hom}_{\Gamma (w/v)}(M_E',E_w^\times )\to \mathrm{Hom}_{\Gamma (w/v)}(M_E',\mathbb {Z})\) for \(\mathcal G_1\) with the corresponding sequence for \(T\), so it suffices to show that \(\ell _w\) is surjective for \(\mathcal G_1\). Since \(M_E'\) is free, applying \(\mathrm{Hom}(M_E',-)\) to \(1\to \mathcal{O}_w^\times \to E_w^\times \xrightarrow {\mathrm{ord}_w}\mathbb {Z}\to 0\) gives a short exact sequence of \(\Gamma (w/v)\)-modules, so the cokernel of \(\ell _w\) embeds in \(H^1(\Gamma (w/v),\mathrm{Hom}(M_E',\mathcal{O}_w^\times ))\). Evaluation at \((v_1^\vee ,v_2^\vee )\) identifies this module with \(\mathcal{O}_w^\times \times \mathcal{O}_w^\times \), on which \(\gamma (x,y)=(\gamma y,\gamma x)\). The map \((x,y)\mapsto (x,\gamma y)\) carries it isomorphically onto \(\mathcal{O}_w^\times \times \mathcal{O}_w^\times \) with \(\gamma \) swapping the two factors. This module is coinduced from the trivial subgroup, so its \(H^1\) vanishes by Shapiro’s lemma.

A support function is a continuous \(\Gamma \)-invariant function \(\varphi \colon \mathfrak t_E\to \mathbb {C}\) that is linear on each cone of the fan of \(S\). For \(x=(x_w)_w\in T(\mathbb {A}_E)\) the toric height is \(H(x,\varphi )=\prod _{w\in Pl_E}\bigl(q_w^{-\varphi (\ell _w(x_w))}\bigr)^{1/[E:\mathbb {Q}]}\), where \(Pl_E\) is the set of places of \(E\), \(q_w=N(w)\) for finite \(w\), and \(q_w=e^2\) at the complex place. For a place \(v\) of \(\mathbb {Q}\) let \(q_v=p\) if \(v=p\) and \(q_\infty =e\), and let \(e_v\) be the ramification index of a place of \(E\) over \(v\) (\(e_\infty =1\)). If \(x=(x_v)_v\in T(\mathbb {A})\) is embedded diagonally in \(T(\mathbb {A}_E)\), then \(\varphi (\ell _w(x_v))\) does not depend on the place \(w\) over \(v\), and the following holds.

\begin{equation*} H(x,\varphi ) = \prod _{v \in Pl_\mathbb {Q}} q_v^{-\frac{1}{e_v}\varphi (\ell _w(x_v)) } \end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

If \(v\) splits in \(E\), its two places are \(w\) and \(\gamma w\), and \(\ell _{\gamma w}(x_v)=\gamma \, \ell _w(x_v)\). Since \(\varphi \) is \(\Gamma \)-invariant (Supporting lemma 4), both places give the same value \(\varphi (\ell _w(x_v))\). Let \(f_v=[E_w:\mathbb {Q}_v]/e_v\), which is the residue degree for finite \(v\), with \(f_\infty =2\), and let \(g_v\) be the number of places of \(E\) over \(v\). Then \(\prod _{w\mid v}q_w=q_v^{f_vg_v}\), so, grouping the factors of \(H(x,\varphi )\) by the place \(v\) below \(w\), the factors over \(v\) multiply to \(q_v^{-\varphi (\ell _w(x_v))\, f_vg_v/[E:\mathbb {Q}]}\). As \(e_vf_vg_v=[E:\mathbb {Q}]=2\), this is \(q_v^{-\varphi (\ell _w(x_v))/e_v}\). Concretely, a split prime \(p\) contributes two factors \(p^{-\varphi /2}\), an inert prime one factor \((p^2)^{-\varphi /2}\), the ramified prime one factor \(3^{-\varphi /2}\), and the complex place one factor \((e^2)^{-\varphi /2}\).

We use the lattices of Proposition 1. For \(m\in M_E\) we write \(\chi ^m\) for the corresponding character of \(T_E\), so \(\chi ^{m+n}=\chi ^m\chi ^n\). The coordinates \(X,Y,Z\) on \(\mathbb {P}^2\) are those of the group algebra element \(X+Y\sigma +Z\sigma ^2\), where \(\sigma \) generates \(C_3\), and \(\sqrt{-27}\) denotes \(3(1+2\zeta )\).

Let \(\mathbf1\) denote the global section in \(H^0(S_E,\mathcal O(3D_0))\) associated to the effective Cartier divisor \(3D_0\), namely the image of \(1\) under the natural inclusion \(\mathcal O_{S_E}\hookrightarrow \mathcal O_{S_E}(3D_0)\). The space \(H^0(S_E,\mathcal O(3D_0))\) is spanned over \(E\) by the following four weight vectors:

\begin{align*} & \mathbf1,\qquad (1-3e_2e_1^{-2})\mathbf1, \\ & (e_1^3-\tfrac 92 e_1 e_2+ \tfrac {27}{2} e_3 + \tfrac {\sqrt{-27}}{2} \sqrt{\mathrm{disc}}) e_1^{-3}\mathbf1,\qquad (e_1^3-\tfrac 92 e_1 e_2+ \tfrac {27}{2} e_3 - \tfrac {\sqrt{-27}}{2} \sqrt{\mathrm{disc}}) e_1^{-3}\mathbf1 \end{align*}

where \(\sqrt{\mathrm{disc}} \mathrel {:=}(X-Z)(Y-X)(Z-Y)\). The associated characters of \(T_E\) are, respectively,

\begin{equation*} 1,\quad \chi ^{a'+b'},\quad \chi ^{2a'+b'},\quad \chi ^{2b'+a'} \end{equation*}

where \(a' = 2v_1^\vee - v_2^\vee \) and \(b' = 2v_2^\vee - v_1^\vee \) in the character lattice \(M_E = X^\ast T_E\) and \((v_1^\vee ,v_2^\vee )\) is the dual basis to \((v_1,v_2)\).

Proof in Lean ▼

Let \(\varphi _0\) be the support function of \(D_0\). In the coordinates \(n=n_1v_1+n_2v_2\) on \(\mathfrak t_E\) it is \(\varphi _0(n)=\min (0,n_1,n_2)\): it equals \(0\), \(v_2^\vee \) and \(v_1^\vee \) on the cones spanned by \(\{ v_1,v_2\} \), \(\{ v_1,v_0\} \) and \(\{ v_2,v_0\} \), where \(v_0=-v_1-v_2\). By the weight decomposition [ 18 , § 3.4, p. 66 ] , \(H^0(S_E,\mathcal O(3D_0))\cong \bigoplus _{m\in P\cap M_E}E\chi ^m\), where \(P=\{ m\in M_E\otimes \mathbb {R}: m\geq 3\varphi _0\} \). Writing \(m=m_1v_1^\vee +m_2v_2^\vee \), \(P\) is the triangle \(m_1,m_2\geq 0\), \(m_1+m_2\leq 3\), and \(m\in M_E\) exactly when \(m_1,m_2\in \mathbb {Z}\) and \(m(\omega )=(2m_1+m_2)/3\in \mathbb {Z}\), that is, \(m_1\equiv m_2\pmod3\). So \(P\cap M_E\) consists of \(0\), \(v_1^\vee +v_2^\vee =a'+b'\), \(3v_1^\vee =2a'+b'\) and \(3v_2^\vee =a'+2b'\), and \(h^0(S_E,\mathcal O(3D_0))=4\). The weight vectors are the twists \(\chi ^m\mathbf1\). Equations 7 and 8 express \(\chi ^{a'+b'}\) and \(\chi ^{a'+2b'}\) through \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) and \(\sqrt{\mathrm{disc}}\), and \(\chi ^{2a'+b'}=\gamma \chi ^{a'+2b'}\) is obtained by changing the sign of \(\sqrt{-27}\).

Let \(C_3\) permute \(X,Y,Z\) cyclically. The complement of \(\mathscr {D}_0\) in the integral model \(\mathscr {S}\) is \(\operatorname {Spec}\mathbb {Z}[X/\varepsilon ,Y/\varepsilon ]^{C_3}\), and a rational point \(P\) of \(S\setminus D_0\) is integral if every element of \(\mathbb {Z}[X/\varepsilon ,Y/\varepsilon ]^{C_3}\) takes an integer value at \(P\).

Write \(e_2,e_3,\delta \) for the dehomogenized invariants

\[ e_2=\frac{XY+YZ+ZX}{\varepsilon ^2},\qquad e_3=\frac{XYZ}{\varepsilon ^3},\qquad \delta =\frac{X^2Y+Y^2Z+Z^2X}{\varepsilon ^3}. \]

Then

\begin{equation*} \mathbb {Z}[X/\varepsilon ,Y/\varepsilon ]^{C_3} =\mathbb {Z}[e_2,e_3,\delta ]. \end{equation*}

A rational point \(P\) of \(S\setminus D_0\) is integral if and only if \(e_2(P),e_3(P)\in \mathbb {Z}\).

Proof in Lean ▼

The equality of rings is Equation 9. Put \(\delta '=(XY^2+YZ^2+ZX^2)/\varepsilon ^3\). Then \(\delta +\delta '=e_2-3e_3\) and \(\delta \delta '=e_2^3+e_3-6e_2e_3+9e_3^2\), so \(\delta \) is a root of the monic quadratic \(t^2-(e_2-3e_3)t+(e_2^3+e_3-6e_2e_3+9e_3^2)\) over \(\mathbb {Z}[e_2,e_3]\). If \(e_2(P),e_3(P)\in \mathbb {Z}\), then \(\delta (P)\) is a rational number integral over \(\mathbb {Z}\), hence an integer. By the equality of rings every invariant is an integer polynomial in \(e_2,e_3,\delta \), so it takes an integer value at \(P\). The converse holds because \(e_2\) and \(e_3\) are invariants.

Here \(C_3\) permutes \(X,Y,Z\) cyclically, and \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) are the elementary symmetric polynomials in \(X,Y,Z\).

The four generators are invariant, which gives the reverse inclusion.

The characteristic polynomial of \((K/\mathbb {Q},x)\) is \(\prod _{g\in C_3}(t-gx)\); its \(t^2\)-coefficient is \(-1\) because \(x\) has trace one. A rational point of \(T\) is integral if it is integral as a point of \(S\setminus D_0\) in the sense of Lemma 2. Since \(3D_0\) is Cartier, \(H(\cdot ,\mathcal O(D_0))=H(\cdot ,\mathcal O(3D_0))^{1/3}\), where \(H(\cdot ,\mathcal O(3D_0))\) is the toric height (Equation 5) for the support function of \(3D_0\).

The characteristic polynomial \(f = t^3-t^2+at+b \in \mathbb {Q}[t]\) of a rational point \((K/\mathbb Q,x) \in T(\mathbb Q)\) has integer coefficients if and only if \((K/\mathbb Q,x)\) is integral. For any integral rational point \((K/\mathbb {Q},x)\) on \(T\),

\begin{equation*} H((K/\mathbb Q,x),\mathcal O(D_0)) = \sqrt{1-3a}. \end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

By [ 26 , Prop. 2.5 ] the unit \(x+(\sigma ^2x)\sigma +(\sigma x)\sigma ^2\) of the group algebra over \(K\) maps to \(P=(K/\mathbb {Q},x)\), so \(X/\varepsilon \), \(Y/\varepsilon \) and \(Z/\varepsilon \) take the values \(x\), \(\sigma ^2x\) and \(\sigma x\) at \(P\). Hence \(e_2(P)=a\) and \(e_3(P)=-b\), and Lemma 2 gives the first assertion. For the height, Equation 11 writes each local factor of \(H(P,\mathcal O(3D_0))\) as a maximum over the four weight vectors of Lemma 1. At an integral point every finite factor is \(1\), and Equation 12 gives the factor at the complex place. Let \(z_\pm =1-\tfrac 92e_2+\tfrac {27}2e_3\pm \tfrac {\sqrt{-27}}2\sqrt{\mathrm{disc}}\), evaluated at \(P\); these are the values of \(\chi ^{2a'+b'}\) and \(\chi ^{a'+2b'}\). Since \((2a'+b')+(a'+2b')=3(a'+b')\) and \(z_-=\overline{z_+}\), we get \(|z_+|^2=z_+z_-=(1-3a)^3\). So \(1-3a\geq 0\); as \(1-3a\) is an integer congruent to \(1\) modulo \(3\), in fact \(1-3a\geq 1\), and the maximum in Equation 12 is \((1-3a)^3\). Thus \(H(P,\mathcal O(3D_0))=(1-3a)^{3/2}\) and \(H(P,\mathcal O(D_0))=\sqrt{1-3a}\).

Since \(\langle m,\ell _w(P)\rangle =\mathrm{ord}_w\chi ^m(P)\), we have \(|\chi ^m(P)|_w=q_w^{-\langle m,\ell _w(P)\rangle }\), so \(q_w^{-3\varphi _0(\ell _w(P))}=\max _m|\chi ^m(P)|_w\) (Supporting lemma 8). On the chart \(e_1=1\), Equations 7 and 8 give \(\chi ^{a'+b'}(P)=1-3e_2\) and give \(\chi ^{a'+2b'}(P)\) and its conjugate \(\chi ^{2a'+b'}(P)\) as the third and fourth entries. Raising to the power \(1/[E:\mathbb {Q}]\) gives the display.

Let \(P=(K/\mathbb {Q},x)\) be an integral rational point of \(T\) with characteristic polynomial \(f=t^3-t^2+at+b\in \mathbb {Z}[t]\). Write \(H_{\mathrm{tor}}(f)=H(P,\mathcal O(D_0))\) for its toric height and set \(h(f)=\max (|a|^{1/2},|b|^{1/3})\), so that \(H(f)=\max \{ 1,h(f)\} \). Then \(a\leq 0\) and \(h(f)^2=-a, H_{\mathrm{tor}}(f)^2=1+3h(f)^2\) so for every \(B\geq 0\),

\begin{equation*} h(f)\leq B \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad H_{\mathrm{tor}}(f)\leq \sqrt{1+3B^2}. \end{equation*}

In particular, \(H_{\mathrm{tor}}(f)\asymp H(f)\). The only polynomial here with \(h(f)=0\) is \(t^3-t^2\), for which \(H_{\mathrm{tor}}(f)=1\).

Proof in Lean ▼

Proposition 2 gives \(H_{\mathrm{tor}}(f)^2=1-3a{\gt}0\), so \(a\leq 0\) because \(a\in \mathbb {Z}\). The algebra \(K\) is \(\mathbb {Q}^3\) or a cyclic cubic field; in the second case complex conjugation is trivial on \(K\) because \(C_3\) has no element of order two. So the roots \(r_1,r_2,r_3\) of \(f\) are real. Write \(A=-a\). Then \(r_1^2+r_2^2+r_3^2=(r_1+r_2+r_3)^2-2a=1+2A\) and \(r_1r_2r_3=-b\), so AM–GM applied to the \(r_i^2\) gives \(b^2\leq ((1+2A)/3)^3\). If \(A\geq 1\), then \(1+2A\leq 3A\), so \(|b|^{2/3}\leq A\); if \(A=0\), then \(b^2\leq 1/27\), so \(b=0\). In both cases \(h(f)^2=-a\). Substituting into \(H_{\mathrm{tor}}(f)^2=1-3a\) gives \(H_{\mathrm{tor}}(f)^2=1+3h(f)^2\), from which the equivalence follows; \(h(f)=0\) forces \(a=b=0\).
