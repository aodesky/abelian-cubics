8 Geometry of the quotient surface

Let \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) be the elementary symmetric polynomials in \(X,Y,Z\), and put \(\delta =X^2Y+Y^2Z+Z^2X\). The integral quotient is \(\mathscr {S}=\operatorname {Proj}(\mathbb {Z}[X,Y,Z]^{C_3})\), with generic fiber \(S\). We describe its cubic embedding and its minimal resolution.

Proposition 9 The invariant cubic model
✓
#

  • CubicTargets.SurfaceGeometry.SurfaceIntegralCubic.Claim
  • Cubic.SurfaceGeometry.SurfaceIntegralCubic.«prop:surfaceIntegralCubic»

The invariant cubics

\[ A=e_1^3,\qquad B=e_1e_2,\qquad C=e_3,\qquad D=\delta \]

identify \(\mathscr {S}\) with the cubic hypersurface in \(\mathbb {P}^3_\mathbb {Z}\) defined by

\begin{equation*} F(A,B,C,D):=B^3+AD^2-ABD+3ACD+A^2C-6ABC+9AC^2=0. \end{equation*}

The divisor \(\mathscr {D}_0=\{ e_1=0\} \) corresponds to the line \(\{ A=B=0\} \) in these coordinates. On the generic fiber,

\begin{equation*} 3D_0\sim -K,\qquad \mathcal{O}(3D_0)\cong \mathcal{O}(1),\qquad (-K)^2=3. \end{equation*}

In particular, this cubic embedding is anticanonical.

Proof in Lean ▼

Let \(R=\mathbb {Z}[X,Y,Z]^{C_3}\). It is generated by \(e_1,e_2,e_3,\delta \), and Equation 42 generates all relations among them. Since \(\operatorname {Proj}R\) equals \(\operatorname {Proj}\) of its third Veronese subring, we pass to that subring. The cubic \(F=0\) of Equation 40 is then the only relation among \(A,B,C,D\), which gives the embedding. Setting \(e_1=0\) leaves the ring \(\mathbb {Z}[C,D]\), so \(\mathscr {D}_0\) is the line \(A=B=0\). On the generic fiber, the hyperplane section \(A=0\) is \(3D_0\), and adjunction gives \(\mathcal{O}(K)\cong \mathcal{O}(-1)\); this proves Equation 41.

\begin{equation*} F(A,B,C,D):=B^3+AD^2-ABD+3ACD+A^2C-6ABC+9AC^2=0. \end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

The third Veronese subring of \(\mathbb {Z}[e_1,e_2,e_3]\) is spanned by the monomials \(e_1^ie_2^je_3^k\) with \(i\equiv j\pmod3\). Writing \(i=3p+r\) and \(j=3q+r\) with \(0\le r{\lt}3\) gives \(e_1^ie_2^je_3^k=A^pB^rh^qC^k\), where \(h=e_2^3\). These monomials with \(r{\lt}3\) are distinct, so \(B^3=Ah\) generates all relations among \(A,B,C,h\). Adjoining \(D=\delta \), Equation 42 reads \(D^2-(B-3C)D+h+AC-6BC+9C^2=0\). Solving it for \(h\) and substituting into \(B^3=Ah\) gives \(F=0\).

Proof in Lean ▼

On the generic fiber the hyperplane \(A=0\) cuts out \(B^3=0\), whose support is the line \(D_0=\{ A=B=0\} \). In the chart \(C=1\), its local ring at the generic point of \(D_0\) is \(\mathbb {Q}(D)[B]/(B^3)\), of length \(3\), so the hyperplane class is \(3D_0\). Adjunction for a cubic surface in \(\mathbb {P}^3\) gives \(\mathcal{O}(K)\cong (\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb {P}^3}(-4)\otimes \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb {P}^3}(3))|_S=\mathcal{O}(-1)\), so \(3D_0\sim -K\). Two general hyperplanes meet in a line, which meets the cubic in three points, so \((-K)^2=3\).

Proof in Lean ▼

Put \(\delta '=XY^2+YZ^2+ZX^2\). Expanding gives \(\delta +\delta '=e_1e_2-3e_3\) and \(\delta \delta '=e_2^3+e_1^3e_3-6e_1e_2e_3+9e_3^2\), so \(\delta \) is a root of \(t^2-(\delta +\delta ')t+\delta \delta '\). To show that this relation generates all others, use it to reduce any relation to the form \(a+b\delta =0\) with \(a,b\in \mathbb {Z}[e_1,e_2,e_3]\). The transposition \(X\leftrightarrow Y\) fixes \(a,b\) and exchanges \(\delta ,\delta '\), so \(b(\delta -\delta ')=0\). Since \(\delta \neq \delta '\), we get \(b=0\) and then \(a=0\).

Proposition 10 The standard rational form
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.SurfaceGeometry.SurfaceRationalForm.Claim
  • Cubic.SurfaceGeometry.SurfaceRationalForm.«prop:surfaceRationalForm»

Over \(\mathbb {Z}[1/6]\), \(\mathscr {S}\) is isomorphic to the cubic hypersurface

\begin{equation*} x_0(x_1^2+3x_2^2)=x_3^3 \end{equation*}

in \(\mathbb {P}_{\mathbb {Z}[1/6]}^3\). Its generic fiber \(S\) is a del Pezzo surface of degree \(3\) and geometric Picard rank \(1\). Its three geometric singularities are of type \(\mathbf A_2\): one is rational, and the other two are conjugate over \(E=\mathbb {Q}(\sqrt{-3})\). Over \(E\), the coordinates \(x_1'=x_1+\sqrt{-3}x_2\) and \(x_2'=x_1-\sqrt{-3}x_2\) give the split toric cubic \(x_0x_1'x_2'=x_3^3\). In these coordinates, \(D_0=\{ x_0=x_3=0\} \).

Proof in Lean ▼

Apply the linear change of coordinates of Equation 44 to the cubic \(F\) of Proposition 9. Its determinant is \(-3^5/2\), a unit in \(\mathbb {Z}[1/6]\), and it transforms \(27F\) into \(x_0(x_1^2+3x_2^2)-x_3^3\). Over \(E\), factoring \(x_1^2+3x_2^2=x_1'x_2'\) gives \(x_0x_1'x_2'=x_3^3\). Its singular points are the three coordinate vertices \([1:0:0:0]\) and \([0:\pm \sqrt{-3}:1:0]\), each with local equation \(xy=z^3\), which is an \(\mathbf A_2\) singularity. The anticanonical class is ample by Proposition 9. The fan of \(S_E\) has three rays in a lattice of rank \(2\), so the geometric Picard rank is \(3-2=1\). The equations of \(D_0\) follow from \(x_0=A\) and \(x_3=A-3B\). In Lean, the coordinate isomorphism is proved over \(\mathbb {Z}[1/6]\) and the remaining assertions on the rational fiber.

Proposition 11 The resolution and boundary
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.SurfaceGeometry.SurfaceResolvedBoundary.Claim
  • Cubic.SurfaceGeometry.SurfaceResolvedBoundary.«prop:surfaceResolvedBoundary»

The surface \(S\) admits a minimal resolution \(\pi \colon X\to S\) over \(\mathbb {Q}\). Put \(D_{\mathrm{red}}=(\pi ^{-1}D_0)_{\mathrm{red}}\). Then \(X\) is a weak del Pezzo surface of degree \(3\), of geometric Picard rank \(7\). The geometric components of \(D_{\mathrm{red}}\) form a chain with self-intersections

\begin{equation*} (-2)-(-2)-(-1)-(-2)-(-2). \end{equation*}

The divisor \(L=-K_X-D_{\mathrm{red}}\) is nef and not numerically zero, but is not big. More precisely,

\begin{equation*} K_X\cdot D_{\mathrm{red}}=-1,\qquad D_{\mathrm{red}}^2=-1,\qquad L^2=0. \end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

Over \(E\), \(S\) is the toric surface whose fan has, in the basis \((\omega ,\gamma \omega )\) of \(N_E\) (Equation 13), the rays \(v_0=(-1,-1)\), \(v_1=(2,-1)\), \(v_2=(-1,2)\). Insert the rays \((0,-1),(1,-1)\) between \(v_0\) and \(v_1\), \((1,0),(0,1)\) between \(v_1\) and \(v_2\), and \((-1,1),(-1,0)\) between \(v_2\) and \(v_0\) [ 18 , § 2.6 ] . Consecutive rays have determinant \(1\), so the new toric surface \(X\) is smooth and projective. The subdivision is Galois-stable, so \(\pi \) is defined over \(\mathbb {Q}\). The neighbors of each inserted ray sum to twice that ray, so every exceptional curve is a \((-2)\)-curve; hence \(\pi \) is minimal [ 19 , Theorems V.5.4 and V.5.7 ] . The inserted rays lie on the segments joining the original rays, so \(\pi \) is crepant and \(-K_X=\pi ^*(-K_S)\) is nef and big with square \(3\). The nine rays give Picard rank \(9-2=7\). Equations 45 and 46 give the boundary chain and the intersection numbers. Since all nine toric divisors sum to \(-K_X\), \(L\) is linearly equivalent to the complementary chain \(D_{v_1},D_{(1,0)},D_{(0,1)},D_{v_2}\), with self-intersections \(-1,-2,-2,-1\). It meets each of its own components in \(0\), each end of \(D_{\mathrm{red}}\) in \(1\), and the other three components of \(D_{\mathrm{red}}\) in \(0\). Torus-invariant curves generate the cone of curves, so \(L\) is nef and not numerically zero. A nef divisor is big exactly when its square is positive, and \(L^2=0\).

Here \(k=2\) for the four inserted rays, and \((-1,0)+(0,-1)=v_0\) gives \(k=1\) for \(v_0\).

\begin{equation*} K_X\cdot D_{\mathrm{red}}=-1,\qquad D_{\mathrm{red}}^2=-1,\qquad L^2=0. \end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

Each component \(C\) of \(D_{\mathrm{red}}\) is a smooth rational curve, so adjunction gives \(K_X\cdot C=-2-C^2\). By Equation 45, this is \(0\) for the four \((-2)\)-curves and \(-1\) for the middle curve, so \(K_X\cdot D_{\mathrm{red}}=-1\). The chain has four adjacent pairs, each meeting once, so \(D_{\mathrm{red}}^2=-9+2\cdot 4=-1\). Finally \(L^2=K_X^2+2K_X\cdot D_{\mathrm{red}}+D_{\mathrm{red}}^2=3-2-1=0\), using \(K_X^2=3\).
