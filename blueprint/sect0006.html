7 Counting arbitrary trace

Let \(F_{\mathrm{mon}}\) be the monic integral cubics with Galois group \(C_3\), let \(F_s\) be those of trace \(s\), and put \(n(f)=a_1^2-3a_2\). We use

\[ A_1(X)=\# \{ t^3-t^2+at+b\in F_1:1-3a\leq X\} , \]
\[ A_0(X)=\# \{ t^3-Nt+c\in F_0:1\leq N\leq X\} . \]

For \(N\geq 1\), let \(r(N)=\# \{ c\in \mathbb {Z}:4N^3-27c^2\text{ is a square}\} \). The polynomial \(Q\) is as in Corollary 1. Summing the trace-zero and trace-one counts over \(s\) proves Theorem 1.

Lemma 12 Reducing the trace

Here \(H(f)\) is the root height, and for \(g=t^3-t^2+at+b\in F_1\), \(H_{\mathrm{tor}}(g)=\sqrt{1-3a}\) is the toric height of Lemma 3.

For \(s\in \mathbb {Z}\), define \(\phi _s\) on \(F_s\) by

\[ \phi _s(f)(t)= \begin{cases} f(t+\tfrac {s}{3}),& s\equiv 0\pmod3,\\ f(t+\tfrac {s-1}{3}),& s\equiv 1\pmod3,\\ -f(-t+\tfrac {s+1}{3}),& s\equiv -1\pmod3. \end{cases} \]

Then \(\phi _s\) is a bijection from \(F_s\) to \(F_0\) if \(3\mid s\), and from \(F_s\) to \(F_1\) if \(3\nmid s\), and it preserves \(n\). For \(f\in F_s\), put \(n=n(f)\). Then \(n{\gt}0\), and

\[ H(f)\leq H \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad |s|\leq H\ \text{ and }\ n\leq s^2+3H^2. \]

If \(3\nmid s\), then \(H_{\mathrm{tor}}(\phi _s(f))^2=n\). If \(3\mid s\), then \(\phi _s(f)=t^3-\frac n3t+c\) for some \(c\in \mathbb {Z}\).

Proof in Lean ▼

The three substitutions send trace \(s\) to \(0\), \(1\) and \(1\). On roots they and their inverses are integer translations and sign changes, so they preserve irreducibility and the Galois group, which gives the bijections. A cyclic cubic field is totally real, so the roots \(r_1,r_2,r_3\) of \(f\) are real and \(n=\frac12\bigl((r_1-r_2)^2+(r_2-r_3)^2+(r_3-r_1)^2\bigr){\gt}0\); the substitutions change root differences only by sign, so they preserve \(n\). For \(g\in F_1\), \(n(g)=1-3a=H_{\mathrm{tor}}(g)^2\) by Lemma 3, and for a trace-zero polynomial \(n\) is \(-3\) times the linear coefficient. For the height, if \(|a_1|\leq H\) and \(|a_2|\leq H^2\) then \(r_1^2+r_2^2+r_3^2=a_1^2-2a_2\leq 3H^2\), and the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality gives \(|a_3|=|r_1r_2r_3|\leq H^3\), so the bound on \(a_3\) is automatic. Since \(a_2=(s^2-n)/3\) and \(n{\gt}0\), the conditions \(|s|\leq H\) and \(|a_2|\leq H^2\) are equivalent to \(|s|\leq H\) and \(n\leq s^2+3H^2\).

For every \(\varepsilon {\gt}0\), the following asymptotic holds as \(X\to \infty \).

\begin{equation*} A_1(X) =\frac12XQ\left(\tfrac 12\log X\right) -\frac{\pi }{9\sqrt3}X +O_\varepsilon (X^{1/2+\varepsilon }). \end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

For \(f=t^3-t^2+at+b\in F_1\), Lemma 3 gives \(H_{\mathrm{tor}}(f)^2=1-3a\), so \(A_1(X)\) counts the \(f\in F_1\) with \(H_{\mathrm{tor}}(f)\leq \sqrt X\). This is the left-hand side of Equation 35, which gives the formula. Equivalently, it is the count of Theorem 2 at root height \(H\) with \(X=1+3H^2\).

The proof assumes Poisson summation (Equation 15) and the Chandrasekharan–Narasimhan estimate (Cited theorem 2).

Lemma 13 Trace-zero coefficients

Here \(d_n\) are the coefficients of \(Z_0(z)=\sum _{n\geq 1}d_nn^{-z}\), given explicitly in Remark 3.

Let \(e\) be the exponent of \(3\) in \(N\), and write \(N=3^eM\) with \(3\nmid M\). Then

\[ r(N)= \begin{cases} d_M,& e=0,\\ 3d_M+1,& N=3u^2\text{ for some }u\in \mathbb {Z}_{{\gt}0},\\ 3d_M,& \text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]
Proof in Lean ▼

Let \(\zeta \) be a primitive cube root of unity, \(\mathcal{O}_E=\mathbb {Z}[\zeta ]\) and \(\lambda =1-\zeta \). If \(\delta \geq 0\) and \(\delta ^2=4N^3-27c^2\), then \(\delta \equiv c\pmod2\) and \(\frac{\delta +3c}2+3c\zeta \in \mathcal{O}_E\) has norm \(N^3\); conversely \(a+b\zeta \) of norm \(N^3\) with \(3\mid b\) gives \(c=b/3\) and \(\delta =|2a-b|\). A value \(c\) with \(\delta {\gt}0\) has two preimages and one with \(\delta =0\) has one. Since \(\mathcal{O}_E\) has class number one, the principal ideals of norm \(N^3\) number \(d_M\): \(3k+1\) choices at a split prime \(p^k\parallel M\), one or none at an inert prime \(q^k\parallel M\) as \(k\) is even or odd, and one at \(\lambda \). If \(e=0\), a generator is a unit modulo \(3\), the six roots of unity map bijectively onto \((\mathcal{O}_E/3\mathcal{O}_E)^\times \), and only the classes \(\pm 1\) have \(\zeta \)-coefficient divisible by \(3\), so two of the six generators qualify and \(r(N)=d_M\). If \(e\geq 1\), every generator is divisible by \(\lambda ^{3e}\), and \(\lambda ^3\in 3\mathcal{O}_E\), so all six qualify. Finally \(\delta =0\) occurs exactly when \(N=3u^2\), with \(c=\pm 2u^3\), which gives the extra \(1\).

The proof assumes Poisson summation (Equation 15).

Proposition 8 The trace-zero count
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.Counting.GeneralMonic.TraceZeroAsymptoticClaim
  • Cubic.Counting.TraceZeroAsymptotic.«prop:traceZeroAsymptotic»

The constant \(\alpha \) is defined in Equation 1.

For every \(\varepsilon {\gt}0\),

\[ A_0(X) =\frac52XQ\left(\tfrac 12\log X\right) -\left(\frac{\alpha }{4}\log 3+\frac{\pi }{3\sqrt3}\right)X +O_\varepsilon (X^{1/2+\varepsilon }). \]
Proof in Lean ▼

The polynomials \(t^3-Nt+c\) with square discriminant \(4N^3-27c^2\) are counted by \(r(N)\). Grouping \(N=3^eM\), Lemma 13 gives \(\sum _{N\leq X}r(N)=\sum _{n\leq X}d_n+3\sum _{e\geq 1}\sum _{n\leq X/3^e}d_n+O(X^{1/2})\), the error coming from the \(N=3u^2\). Apply Equation 33 to each inner sum. Since \(Q\) is linear with leading coefficient \(2\alpha /9\), \(Q(\frac12\log X-\frac e2\log 3)=Q(\frac12\log X)-\frac{\alpha e}9\log 3\), and \(\sum _{e\geq 1}3^{1-e}=\frac32\) and \(\sum _{e\geq 1}e\, 3^{1-e}=\frac94\) give \(\frac52XQ(\frac12\log X)-\frac\alpha 4(\log 3)X+O_\varepsilon (X^{1/2+\varepsilon })\). A reducible cubic with square discriminant splits completely. The split \(t^3-Nt+c\) are the \(S_3\)-orbits of \((x,y)\in \mathbb {Z}^2\) with roots \(x,y,-x-y\) and \(N=x^2+xy+y^2\). The ellipse \(x^2+xy+y^2\leq X\) has area \(2\pi X/\sqrt3\) and the nonidentity fixed loci contain \(O(X^{1/2})\) lattice points, so Burnside’s lemma counts \(\frac{\pi }{3\sqrt3}X+O(X^{1/2})\) split cubics. Subtracting them gives the proposition.

The proof assumes Poisson summation (Equation 15) and the Chandrasekharan–Narasimhan estimate (Cited theorem 2).

The main term of Theorem 1 involves two integrals over the normalized trace \(u=s/H\in [-1,1]\).

\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} \int _{-1}^1(3+u^2)\, du& =\frac{20}{3},\\ \int _{-1}^1(3+u^2)\log (3+u^2)\, du & =\frac{40}{3}\log 2+\frac{4\pi \sqrt3}{3}-\frac{76}{9}. \end{aligned}\end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

The first integral is \([3u+u^3/3]_{-1}^1=20/3\). For the second, integrate by parts with \(G(u)=3u+u^3/3\): the boundary term is \(\frac{20}3\log 4\), and the remaining integrand is \(\frac{2uG(u)}{3+u^2}=\frac23(u^2+6)-\frac{12}{u^2+3}\). Its integral over \([-1,1]\) is \(\frac{76}9-\frac{4\pi \sqrt3}{3}\), using \(\int _{-1}^1\frac{du}{u^2+3}=\frac2{\sqrt3}\arctan \frac1{\sqrt3}=\frac{\pi }{3\sqrt3}\).

Summing the main terms of Equation 36 and Proposition 8 over the traces in Equation 37 gives the main term \(H^3P(\log H)\) of Theorem 1. In terms of the polynomial \(Q\) of Corollary 1 and the constant \(\alpha \) of Equation 1, the polynomial \(P\) is as follows.

\begin{equation*} P(X) =\frac{110}{27}Q(X) +\frac{220\alpha }{243}\left(\log 2-\frac{19}{44}\log 3 -\frac{19}{30}+\frac{\pi \sqrt3}{10}\right) -\frac{20\pi \sqrt3}{81}. \end{equation*}
Proof in Lean ▼

For a residue class \(r\) modulo \(3\) and a smooth \(g\), \(\sum _{|s|\leq H,\, s\equiv r\ (3)}g(s/H)=\frac H3\int _{-1}^1g(u)\, du+O_g(1)\). With \(u=s/H\), the inputs are \(3H^2+s^2=H^2(3+u^2)\) for \(A_1\) on two residue classes and \(H^2+s^2/3=H^2(3+u^2)/3\) for \(A_0\) on one. Since \(Q\) is linear with leading coefficient \(2\alpha /9\), expanding \(Q(\log H+\frac12\log (3+u^2))\) and \(Q(\log H+\frac12\log \frac{3+u^2}3)\) gives \(P(X)=\bigl(\frac{11}{18}Q(X)-\frac{19\alpha }{324}\log 3-\frac{\pi }{9\sqrt3}\bigr)\int _{-1}^1(3+u^2)\, du+\frac{11\alpha }{162}\int _{-1}^1(3+u^2)\log (3+u^2)\, du\). Inserting the two integrals of Equation 38 gives the formula.

Remark 4 Translation classes
✓
#

  • CubicTargets.Counting.TranslationClasses.Claim

For \(f=t^3+a_1t^2+a_2t+a_3\) put \(I=a_1^2-3a_2\), \(J=-2a_1^3+9a_1a_2-27a_3\) and \(H_{\mathrm{BS}}(f)=\max \{ |I|^3,J^2/4\} \). The introduction states that, for every \(\varepsilon {\gt}0\), the number of translation classes in \(F_{\mathrm{mon}}\) with \(H_{\mathrm{BS}}(f)\leq H\) is

\[ H^{1/3}P_{\mathrm{BS}}(\log H)+O_\varepsilon (H^{1/6+\varepsilon }), \]

where

\[ P_{\mathrm{BS}}(X)=\frac{11\alpha }{162}\biggl(X+6\mu -6\frac{\zeta '(2)}{\zeta (2)}+6\beta -3-\frac{15}{44}\log 3\biggr)-\frac{\pi }{3\sqrt3}. \]

The constants \(\alpha \), \(\mu \) and \(\beta \) are defined in Equation 1.

We derive the count of translation classes by \(H_{\mathrm{BS}}\) stated in the introduction. The invariant \(I\) there is \(n(f)\). Translating the roots by an integer \(k\) changes the trace by \(3k\) and preserves \(n(f)\), so each translation class in \(F_{\mathrm{mon}}\) has exactly one representative with trace in \(\{ -1,0,1\} \). The map \(f(t)\mapsto -f(-t)\) negates the roots, so it matches the classes of trace \(1\) with those of trace \(-1\) and preserves \(n(f)\). A trace-one polynomial \(t^3-t^2+at+b\) has \(n(f)=1-3a\), and \(t^3-Nt+c\) has \(n(f)=3N\). Hence the number \(C(Y)\) of translation classes with \(I\leq Y\) is

\[ C(Y)=2A_1(Y)+A_0(Y/3). \]

Substituting 36 and Proposition 8, and using that \(Q\) is linear with leading coefficient \(2\alpha /9\), gives

\[ C(Y)=Y\left(\frac{11}{6}Q\left(\tfrac 12\log Y\right) -\frac{19\alpha }{108}\log 3-\frac{\pi }{3\sqrt3}\right) +O_\varepsilon (Y^{1/2+\varepsilon }). \]

Since \(f\) has three distinct real roots, \(4I^3-J^2=27\operatorname {disc}(f){\gt}0\), so \(H_{\mathrm{BS}}(f)=I^3\). Setting \(Y=H^{1/3}\) and substituting the formula for \(Q\) from Corollary 1 gives the stated polynomial \(P_{\mathrm{BS}}\) and error \(O_\varepsilon (H^{1/6+\varepsilon })\).

Proof in Lean ▼

The invariant \(I\) is \(n(f)\). Translating the roots by an integer \(k\) changes the trace by \(3k\) and preserves \(n(f)\), so each translation class has exactly one representative of trace \(-1\), \(0\) or \(1\), and \(f(t)\mapsto -f(-t)\) matches the classes of trace \(1\) with those of trace \(-1\). Since \(t^3-t^2+at+b\) has \(n(f)=1-3a\) and \(t^3-Nt+c\) has \(n(f)=3N\), the number of classes with \(I\leq Y\) is \(C(Y)=2A_1(Y)+A_0(Y/3)\). Equation 36 and Proposition 8, with \(Q(\frac12\log \frac Y3)=Q(\frac12\log Y)-\frac\alpha 9\log 3\), give \(C(Y)=Y\bigl(\frac{11}6Q(\frac12\log Y)-\frac{19\alpha }{108}\log 3-\frac{\pi }{3\sqrt3}\bigr)+O_\varepsilon (Y^{1/2+\varepsilon })\). The roots are real and distinct, so \(4I^3-J^2=27\operatorname {disc}(f){\gt}0\) and \(H_{\mathrm{BS}}(f)=I^3\). Setting \(Y=H^{1/3}\) and inserting \(Q\) from Corollary 1 gives \(P_{\mathrm{BS}}\).

The proof assumes Poisson summation (Equation 15) and the Chandrasekharan–Narasimhan estimate (Cited theorem 2).

Theorem 7 Distribution of the trace
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CubicTargets.Counting.TraceDistribution.Claim
  • Cubic.Counting.TraceDistribution.«thm:traceDistribution»

For \(r\in \{ 0,1,2\} \) and \(-1\leq u_1\leq u_2\leq 1\), let

\[ N_r(H;u_1,u_2)=\# \Bigl\{ f\in F_{\mathrm{mon}}:H(f)\leq H,\ \operatorname {Tr}(f)\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3),\ u_1\leq \frac{\operatorname {Tr}(f)}{H}\leq u_2\Bigr\} . \]

The constant \(\alpha \) is defined in Equation 1.

Uniformly in \(r\), \(u_1\) and \(u_2\), as \(H\to \infty \),

\[ N_r(H;u_1,u_2) =c_r\left(\int _{u_1}^{u_2}(3+u^2)\, du\right)H^3\log H+O(H^3), \qquad c_0=\frac{5\alpha }{81},\quad c_1=c_2=\frac{\alpha }{27}. \]

Consequently, among the polynomials counted in Theorem 1, the proportions with trace congruent to \(0\), \(1\) and \(2\) modulo \(3\) tend to \(5/11\), \(3/11\) and \(3/11\). Within each residue class, \(\operatorname {Tr}(f)/H\) has limiting density \(\frac{3}{20}(3+u^2)\) on \([-1,1]\).

Proof in Lean ▼

Restricting Equation 37 to traces \(s\equiv r\pmod3\) with \(u_1H\leq s\leq u_2H\) writes \(N_r(H;u_1,u_2)\) as a sum of \(A_1(3H^2+s^2)\) if \(r\neq 0\) and of \(A_0(H^2+s^2/3)\) if \(r=0\). Since \(Q\) is linear with leading coefficient \(2\alpha /9\), Equation 36 and Proposition 8 give \(A_1(X)=\frac{\alpha }{18}X\log X+O(X)\) and \(A_0(X)=\frac{5\alpha }{18}X\log X+O(X)\). With \(u=s/H\), both inputs are \(H^2(3+u^2)\) up to the factor \(1/3\), so trace \(s\) contributes \(\frac\alpha 9(3+u^2)H^2\log H+O(H^2)\) if \(r\neq 0\) and \(\frac{5\alpha }{27}(3+u^2)H^2\log H+O(H^2)\) if \(r=0\). The Riemann sum \(\sum _{u_1H\leq s\leq u_2H,\ s\equiv r\ (3)}(3+s^2/H^2)=\frac H3\int _{u_1}^{u_2}(3+u^2)\, du+O(1)\), uniform in \(u_1\), \(u_2\) and \(r\), together with the \(O(H)\) traces, gives the formula. Since \(\int _{-1}^1(3+u^2)\, du=\frac{20}3\), the three classes together give \(\frac{220\alpha }{243}H^3\log H\), the leading term of Theorem 1; dividing gives the proportions \(5/11\), \(3/11\), \(3/11\) and the density \(\frac3{20}(3+u^2)\). In Lean, the limiting density means that \(N_r(H;u_1,u_2)/N_r(H;-1,1)\) tends to \(\int _{u_1}^{u_2}\frac{3}{20}(3+u^2)\, du\).

The proof assumes Poisson summation (Equation 15) and the Chandrasekharan–Narasimhan estimate (Cited theorem 2).
